## Examples

**Future window survives a legacy deadline.** An order's assessment deadline is 16 October, while its separate committed start window includes 1 November. On 1 November, Workflow verifies the AP evidence and final admission; it must not take the legacy unconditional `bindingExpired` unwind solely because 16 October passed. Genuine final-permit expiry still stops new application and retains recovery obligations.

**Draft disappears after payment.** Payment succeeded but the original draft expired before fulfillment. The compensation table may report the draft as absent. The order still owns a paid acquisition and its protection. Workflow recovers/rebinds that acquisition under AP07 or performs the agreed cancellation/remedy; it does not report all obligations settled from draft absence.

**Lost spawn-signal response.** Lifecycle committed the signal, but Workflow did not receive the response. Recover that same signal before dispatch. This behavior is already in #4787; the proposal adds proof that capture/financial recovery cannot be skipped on the same path.

**Postpay.** Accepted credit-service terms authorize service and no immediate debit is required. Workflow follows the applicable process route and passes final financial evidence to Subscriptions. It does not wait for a card authorization that this settlement scheme does not require.

## Abort and recovery branches

Scope the complete legacy overlap-abort branch to a definitively unprotected acquisition: the overlap decision, stop-further-dispatch action, draft void, `overlap-collision` cause and `in_fulfillment → fulfillment_failed` acknowledgement. A declaration near the top of the document must not leave later unconditional abort steps applying to protected acquisitions.

For protected acquisition, validate AP02 own-claim evidence and use AP07 recovery. An owned reservation is not a collision with itself; unknown occupancy or a missing claim read is not proof of an unprotected collision. Keep claim, commitment and financial obligations until authoritative application/capture recovery, effect-owner fencing and the required remedy allow coordinated release. Draft void may finish only its own leg. If the final operational status is failed, this does not erase remaining financial work.

**Example.** Limit one is fully occupied by this order's reservation. The occupancy service times out on re-check after payment. The reviewed design already distinguishes an unavailable read from a proven collision: it defers and, after its bounded retry ladder, aborts with `overlap-read-unevaluable` (slice 04 §4.7), not `overlap-collision`. For the protected-acquisition profile, amend that exhaustion/unwind path to retain the original reservation, commercial and financial recovery obligations until the agreed settlement/release conditions hold. Neither inability to read occupancy nor successful draft void proves the paid claim is safely releasable. Separately, a successful occupancy read must recognize this acquisition's own claim rather than count it as an unrelated collision.

## Operation identity and recovery proposal

**Status — 2026-10-18:** the user requested recording this resolution after discussing lost replies and late requests. This section proposes the shared contract; it does not claim owner adoption, a published SDK, implementation or executed tests. It supplements OW-P04 and OW-P06 without changing acquisition, financial or activation authority.

Workflow already persists its intent before sending it and defines a complete lookup tuple. Its SUB-O13 note identifies a narrower Subscriptions lookup as a remaining gap. The proposal is to use that existing complete identity, rather than introduce another caller-generated operation UUID. An operation's recovery identity must outlive the temporary transport deduplication record whenever recovery or delayed delivery remains possible.

### Identity and lookup

1. The proposed scoped operation identity is `(resource_tenant_id, orderId, orderVersion, orderLineId, wave, intentKind, wave_attempt)`. Both owners must specify the same versioned canonical encoding, including an explicit initial attempt. Workflow persists it and the immutable normalized request/digest before sending. Subscriptions durably associates its request record with that identity before executing effects. Creation, activation, draft void and activated cancellation are distinct actions; the operation identity is neither the process correlation ID nor the engine's invocation/retry identity.
2. Subscriptions exposes authorized lookup by that complete identity, as well as by its returned `transition_request_id`. Both address the same durable operation and return its current authoritative outcome, target reference when known, and reason/evidence needed for recovery. Explicitly extend the reviewed SUB-O13 recovery expectation to retained terminal outcomes as well as pending operations; lookup is not limited to requests that are still non-terminal. A partial tuple must not silently select the latest or an arbitrary operation. A lost server-generated request ID therefore does not prevent recovery.
3. Repeating the same identity and normalized payload returns the retained operation and outcome. A different payload under that identity, including a changed expected subscription revision, conflicts before effects. Changing a transport key must not bypass the existing business deduplication or creation fence. Conflict is not permission to mint another key and repeat the effect.
4. Keep the operation identity distinct from Subscriptions' acquisition/create fence `(order_id, order_version, attempt_id, line_id)`. The SDK must carry an explicit association between them. Do not equate every action's `wave_attempt` with the acquisition's `attempt_id`: activation and compensation must identify the acquisition/draft attempt they act on, while their own action attempts distinguish their commands. A platform task retry changes neither identity.
5. A genuinely new acquisition attempt uses the existing [successor-attempt protocol](../ACTIVATION_PROPOSAL.md#7-boundary-failures-and-recovery): authoritative predecessor settlement, suppression of stale create/activation effects, authorized lineage and exclusive reassociation preserving applicable claims, commercial commitments and financial references. A historically successful create whose draft later expired remains a successful create. It must not be relabelled as never-created; rebuilding it requires the supported successor association, not merely a fresh transport key.
6. Confirmation events echo the operation identity and correlation information. They wake reconciliation; an authoritative status read determines the result. Lookups enforce caller and tenant authorization before disclosure. A denied or hidden lookup provides no evidence of non-application; preserve the platform's nondisclosure policy rather than exposing existence through a new lookup route.

### Absence, expiry and settlement

**Difference from the reviewed Workflow design.** Slice 05 §3.3, D-209 already specifies recovery of an uncertain create by resubmitting the same intent key, obtaining the existing or newly created draft, then voiding it when cancellation is required. That protocol relies on the supported create-deduplication lifetime. The settlement/withdrawal path proposed below is an explicit alternative to that procedure for cancellation, not a claim that the PR has no delayed-request handling. Separately, replacing the post-expiry `not_found → never-dispatched` inference remains necessary for the proposed durable recovery guarantees. Adoption must reconcile the two owner protocols rather than silently assuming Workflow already calls settlement.

**Proposed realization: versioned acquisition-attempt fencing.** The user proposed using versioning to implement the late-create prohibition. Bind creation and cancellation to the same scoped acquisition attempt and its expected generation, including the order version and line identity from the acquisition/create fence above. Order cancellation or supersession must cover every affected line/attempt; closing one line is not evidence that another is fenced. Subscriptions durably records that attempt as closed even when no draft exists. A delayed create for the closed attempt is rejected. Checking the expected generation/closed state and applying creation must serialize with closing the attempt at the Subscriptions effect boundary; a caller-supplied version field or a read before an unguarded write is insufficient. If creation commits first, recover its result and use ordinary compensation; versioning does not erase an applied effect. A closure record blocks future application but does not prove that already dispatched external work is absent or cleaned up; retain that work as pending until authoritative settlement. Service preparation follows the persisted draft/activation operation under the initial-readiness contract, not an assumed pre-draft adapter call.

Reuse the existing acquisition-attempt identity and any compatible generation mechanism; this does not mandate a new identifier or equate the attempt generation with the order version, subscription revision, transport key or each action's `wave_attempt`. A retry retains its original attempt/generation. A new attempt requires the successor protocol above, not an increment that bypasses unresolved work. Closure evidence or an equivalent durable obsolete-generation rejection must survive transport-cache expiry under the retention rules below. This is our proposed implementation direction for owner review, not a delivered contract or a decision attributed to Fable. Exact fields and storage mechanics remain implementation work.

An authorized `not_found` read means only that the lookup found no record at that instant. It neither prevents a delayed request from arriving nor proves that an old effect never occurred after recovery records have been retired. A timeout or unavailable dependency is an unknown outcome, never an absence observation.

Within the supported replay interval, and only while dispatch remains authorized, Workflow may resend the identical operation under the same identity and payload. Subscriptions' atomic deduplication and business fences must serialize this resend with any delayed original. A pending or externally uncertain operation is recovered by status, not replaced with a new attempt.

After the replay guarantee expires, or when cancellation requires a definitive outcome, stop blind resubmission. Use the original operation's durable settlement/withdrawal contract. This is a separately keyed command referring to the original action, not a changed payload under the original action key. Subscriptions serializes it against creation or final application and answers:

| Authoritative result | Workflow action |
| --- | --- |
| Already applied | Recover the original receipt and target. If cancellation is required, issue the separate applicable compensation; withdrawal cannot erase an applied action. |
| Confirmed non-application with a durable fence | Record the terminal outcome. Delayed original requests cannot apply. Any successor still requires the applicable authorization, association and financial guards. |
| Pending or uncertain, including outstanding external cleanup | Retain recovery work and escalate as required. Do not report safe non-application, release protected rights or create a replacement from uncertainty. |

For cancel-before-create, the [Foundation settlement flow](../features/01-foundation.md#withdraw-or-settle-an-uncertain-attempt) records the no-create tombstone before acknowledging settlement. If creation wins the race, settlement returns the existing applied result instead. These outcomes survive restart.

Replace Workflow 05's inference from `not_found` after key expiry to `failed/never-dispatched` with this recovery path. Even confirmed non-application does not prove the request was never sent: record the settled reason accurately. The existing `oss_unconfirmed` label, a timeout or a generic `failed` label must not authorize a fresh attempt unless Subscriptions supplies authoritative settlement of the relevant effect. A process may escalate or stop trying while that effect remains unresolved; its operational status is not settlement evidence.

### Retention and late delivery

Separate bounded transport-key replay from durable business recovery. Retain the compact identity, normalized payload digest, receipt/outcome, acquisition association and required tombstone or generation fence across the agreed retry, hold, recovery and delayed-delivery windows. Pending effects cannot be garbage-collected merely because a transport TTL elapsed. Expiry of a deduplication cache neither authorizes another effect nor changes a known applied or withdrawn outcome to clean absence.

Retiring recovery evidence requires an explicit protocol that rejects obsolete generations at the effect boundary and preserves authoritative archival/recovery evidence. A cancelled attempt must never become admissible again because its tombstone was removed. If historical evidence is unavailable, report the recovery limitation and retain uncertainty rather than infer non-application. This proposal chooses no numeric retention period or unlimited storage policy; the owners must version the retention and retirement contract before delivery.

## Approval valuation basis proposal

**Status — 2026-10-18:** user-authorized proposal extending OW-P07. The reviewed Workflow slice 03 already specifies a Rating-computed, net pre-tax, annualised TCV stored by Orders Lifecycle and forwarded unchanged. Workflow also already delegates the approval-requirement verdict rather than evaluating monetary thresholds itself. Preserve those ownership boundaries. The change proposed here is explicit policy selection of the valuation basis, together with the money representation update; it is not a finding that the existing design has no valuation basis. The preceding Fable consultation covered operation recovery, not this section.

**Proposed rule.** The versioned approval policy declares which value its threshold consumes. For a fixed purchase with a committed term, the proposed default is the total contractual monetary commitment for that term, not the first instalment or an automatically annualised amount. Monthly billing alone does not establish a twelve-month commitment. Other bases, including annualised value, require an explicit policy choice. The policy declares its threshold currency, comparison rule, covered term/horizon and treatment of taxes, discounts, one-time charges and variable components; these cannot change silently between valuation and approval.

The designated valuation owner, Rating for tariff valuation in the reviewed design, calculates the selected commercial value. Orders Lifecycle retains the evidence with the immutable order version. The evidence identifies its source and valuation version, the requested basis, covered term/horizon and components, currency, exact major-unit decimal amount when available, and whether it is a contractual amount, an estimate, an explicit budget or unavailable. A budget is supplied by its authorized owner; Workflow must not invent it from a price or usage forecast. Financial valuation and budget ownership remain external to Workflow.

Workflow forwards the matching evidence to the existing approval authority and records its verdict and policy/evidence references. It performs no tariff arithmetic, currency conversion or threshold evaluation. The evidence must match the order version and policy basis; an amount obtained for another basis is not an interchangeable fallback. An order amendment that changes the valuation or approval context must follow the existing reassessment/re-approval route rather than reuse an old verdict by amount alone.

**Usage-based purchases.** Future usage may have no known total. The policy must explicitly select an available basis, such as an authorized budget or a separately identified estimate with its horizon and assumptions. Neither an estimate nor an approval budget is automatically a contractual minimum, payment authorization, spending cap or resource limit. Any enforcement of a budget needs its own explicit contract. Unavailable required evidence remains unavailable: never coerce it to zero, silently substitute the first bill or return an amount-based approval-not-required verdict. Defer to obtain the evidence or use an explicitly configured non-monetary/manual approval route through the approval authority. A policy that independently requires no approval can still return that verdict; missing cost evidence must not manufacture it.

**Examples (illustrative values).**

| Purchase and policy | Expected interpretation |
| --- | --- |
| PaaS at EUR 100 per month with a fixed twelve-month commitment; policy requires approval above EUR 1,000 using full-term net commitment; no additional charges or discounts | The valuation owner returns EUR 1,200 for the committed term. The approval authority requires approval; Workflow does not compare the EUR 100 instalment instead. |
| PaaS at EUR 100 per month with only one month committed under the same basis | The owner reports the actual one-month commitment. Do not invent EUR 1,200 merely because monthly billing could continue. |
| Usage-based PaaS with unknown future consumption and an explicitly authorized EUR 2,000 budget for a declared horizon; policy selects that budget | Forward the budget as budget evidence. It is neither a forecast of the bill nor a promise to spend EUR 2,000. |
| Usage-based PaaS with no value matching the required policy basis | Obtain evidence or follow the policy's explicit alternative route. Unknown is not zero and cannot silently bypass approval. |

**Owner changes and delivery boundary.** Align Workflow slice 03 request/storage contracts and its upstream requirements with Orders Lifecycle's immutable valuation evidence, Rating's supported basis/representation, and the approval policy adapter's verdict contract. Preserve references-only process-engine inputs and the existing money ADR distinctions between exact value, display and posting estimate. Do not derive cash collection instructions from the approval value. Threshold currency must match the supplied evidence, or an authorized financial owner must supply explicitly converted evidence with its provenance; Workflow never applies an implicit exchange rate.

Planned acceptance covers: full-term versus first-instalment selection; one-month versus annual commitment; PAYG budget versus estimate versus unavailable value; unchanged exact decimal transport; currency/basis mismatch refusing silent comparison; and amended-order evidence invalidating reuse of an unrelated approval verdict. These cases are not executed tests. Exact versioned provider schemas, supported policy bases and reciprocal owner adoption remain delivery prerequisites; no adjacent document or runtime is changed by this proposal.


## BSS Approvals integration and assignee selection

**Status — 2026-10-18:** user-agreed open integration dependency. The current platform provides no agreed role-based approver-selection contract for this workflow. This is not a claim that platform authorization is absent. Labels such as "manager" and "finance" are illustrative business requirements, not implemented role types or permission grants. The initial assignment approach, a named-user criterion in the shared approval library, and RBAC for assignment management are agreed below. The eligible-user source, concrete RBAC permissions and bindings, and integration of decision-time authorization remain OPEN.

**Agreed initial scope.** Start with one predefined stage, "order approval", and one explicitly assigned user/principal for that stage in the seller's tenant configuration. This applies when the approval policy requires approval; it does not make every order require a vote. The stage label is not a platform role and grants no unrelated permissions. Multiple approval stages, role/group resolution and tenant-defined routes are outside this initial scope. Permission to configure or replace the assignee is governed by RBAC and must be enforced by the owning backend in the applicable tenant/seller scope. Concrete permission names, role bindings, validation of eligible users and handling of changes to open requests remain open for implementation design. No seller owner or other actor receives assignment-management authority implicitly. Permission to manage assignments is distinct from permission to approve a request; assigning a user does not create a general platform role.

**Existing boundaries.** The [shared approval library](../../../libs/approval/README.md) supplies units, quorum, generation-aware decisions and separation-of-duties rules. Its owning gear supplies authorization, policy loading, storage, idempotency and events. The [BSS Approvals Inbox](../../../approvals/docs/DESIGN.md) merges owner-held units and forwards votes; it does not select the route or create role assignments. Its current closed kinds cover Pricing and Products. Orders needs an owner source and agreed kind/schema extension before its units can appear there. The reviewed [Workflow approval design](https://github.com/constructorfabric/gears-rust/blob/ce16d8ff2f59e674ca5e36b091588781022348ea/gears/bss/orders-workflow/docs/design/03-approval-execution.md) distinguishes a business `party_ref` from a concrete `assigned_principal`; its inbox filters by the assigned principal, and an unassigned gate goes to operator handling rather than becoming visible to everyone. Do not interpret a party label as a platform role or assume a role/group resolver exists.

**Agreed approval criterion types.** Distinguish two semantic types in the shared approval library: numeric quorum among eligible approvers, and decisions by explicitly named users/principals. Votes by other users cannot substitute for the named users. The initial Orders scope uses exactly one named approver. For a future list of multiple named approvers, whether all are required or a specified subset suffices remains an OPEN product rule; do not infer unanimity, any-one approval or a threshold by default. Concrete type names and schemas are deferred to implementation.

**Agreed named-user approval criterion.** Extend the shared approval library so a unit can require the decision of one explicitly identified user/principal as an alternative to its existing numeric-quorum rule. Workflow supplies the assigned principal when establishing its order-approval unit; the library persists that criterion with the unit and enforces it when accepting the decision. Another user's vote cannot satisfy or decide that unit merely because that user has a general approval permission. Preserve the existing quorum behavior for other consumers; do not replace their policies or infer a named-user criterion from a display label. The assigned user remains subject to the existing author/submitter separation-of-duties restrictions and the owning gear's current tenant-scoped authorization checks. Assignment-management permission does not itself authorize an approval decision.

The owning gear continues to supply authenticated actor identity, enforce RBAC for assignment/replacement, and own storage and execution. The common BSS Approvals Inbox forwards decisions to that owner; it does not become the policy engine. The named-user rule must be enforced in the shared library on the owner-side decision path, not solely by inbox filtering or a Workflow-only check. Assignment replacement must be tied to the unit's version/generation so a stale decision by the former assignee cannot satisfy the changed criterion; exact replacement and vote-invalidation mechanics remain implementation design. Missing or ineligible assignees require explicit handling, not fallback to an unrestricted quorum. This is a proposed library extension, not current behavior or implemented code.

**Proposed integration direction.** For the agreed initial scope, map the single order-approval stage to one library unit, with Workflow owning waiting and continuation. The reviewed design supports multiple stages; mapping each to a separate unit is a possible later extension, outside this initial scope. This is a mapping proposal requiring owner agreement, not implemented integration. A numeric quorum cannot by itself require distinct business capacities: two votes do not prove one manager and one finance decision. For the initial stage, the named-user library criterion supplies the assignee restriction; the owner authorization and assignment-management integration still needs definition. Keep the disclosed stand-in explicitly identifiable while the library binding is unavailable; its unconditional `not_required` result is not evidence that a configured real approval policy was evaluated. Do not enable a profile requiring real approvals through that stand-in or treat an unavailable authority as approval.

**Questions to resolve before real approval routing is enabled:**

- How is the authoritative assignment configuration stored and accessed within the agreed seller/tenant scope? Explicit user/principal assignment is the agreed initial approach; role/group selection and "the submitter's manager" require additional authoritative data and are not assumed.
- Which RBAC permissions and role bindings authorize assignment creation and replacement in the applicable scope, and how are changes versioned and reflected in already-open stages? Delegation rules remain to be defined. An unassigned stage requires explicit handling, never fallback to any caller.
- How does the owner integrate current decision-time permission checks with the library's stored named-user criterion and self-approval restrictions? A display label, historic selection or inbox visibility alone does not authorize a vote.
- How do assignment changes, loss of authority, order-version changes and late decisions interact with the unit generation and cancellation fences? Earlier decisions must not silently authorize changed content or a different stage.
- How are the Workflow stage, library unit, policy version and authoritative decision mapped, and how is the Orders source exposed through the common inbox without duplicating ownership?

This section selects RBAC for assignment-management authorization, without introducing a new role model, role resolver or group membership service, or choosing which concrete roles receive that authority. Defining these contracts is Workflow/approval integration work with the platform authorization and identity owners; it is not a Subscriptions lifecycle responsibility. A later Fable consultation informed the named-user option; the single-stage scope and RBAC direction were subsequently selected by the user. The later named-user library criterion was proposed and selected by the user. Consultation does not settle the remaining authorization or integration details. ## Executable workflow prototype recommendation

**Status — 2026-10-18:** user-approved recommendation for implementation planning, not a new condition for merging the design or a claim that runtime verification has passed. No prototype or tests are delivered by this section.

**Recommendation.** Build a small executable order scenario on the selected Serverless Runtime and its Temporal plugin before expanding the complete process. Use one PaaS order line: create a draft, wait for the planned start, request activation, recover or await its final result, then complete the order. Subscriptions owns service preparation and the initial-readiness gate; Orders waits for its final activation outcome. Use controllable test doubles for Subscriptions and other business services, while exercising the real runtime's calls, timers, event delivery and persistence. A full BSS deployment is unnecessary for this experiment.

**Why this is useful.** The reviewed [process-definition design, sections 3.6 and 4.4–4.6](https://github.com/constructorfabric/gears-rust/blob/ce16d8ff2f59e674ca5e36b091588781022348ea/gears/bss/orders-workflow/docs/design/10-process-definition.md) explicitly presents abridged YAML and leaves runtime-dependent questions open: event retention between competing listeners, behavior after retry exhaustion, re-drive of a failed invocation, and history growth during frequent polling. The abbreviations are documented, not syntax defects. A complete executable definition and observed outcomes would test those assumptions more directly than schema validation alone.

The following are proposed prototype checks, not executed acceptance results:

| Check | Experiment and expected evidence |
| --- | --- |
| Normal completion | Create the draft, wait for the scheduled start and receive an asynchronous activation result. Keep the order unfinished on mere request acceptance; complete it only after the final applied outcome. The Subscriptions double models readiness before initial activation. |
| Lost response | Let the downstream double apply creation or activation, then drop its response. Recover the original operation using its persisted identity; show that no duplicate subscription or effect is created. |
| Worker restart | Restart the runtime worker while waiting and around a call whose effect may already have committed. Resume the same process with its recorded state and recover uncertain outcomes rather than starting a second purchase. |
| Cancellation race | Deliver cancellation between listener registrations and concurrently with activation confirmation. Demonstrate that the durable cancellation request is eventually processed and that all applied or late effects are accounted for through Subscriptions before operational cancellation completes. |
| Retry exhaustion and recovery | Keep a dependency unavailable beyond the configured retry budget, then restore it. Record the actual invocation state, operator task and supported continuation or compensation path. Verify that the chosen behavior is explicit, that an unknown outcome is never treated as non-execution, and that resumption preserves operation identity. |
| Long waiting period | Measure calls and history growth under the intended polling frequencies for a month-long scenario, including extended approval or activation waits. Compare with the actual runtime limits and demonstrate any required continuation or history-management mechanism. Shortened timers or projections may support the experiment, but must be reported as such, not as proof of a month-long production run. |

**Suggested output.** Retain the complete YAML, runtime/plugin versions, test-double behavior, failure-injection points and observed outcomes. Report which checks passed, failed or remain unsupported, together with history/call measurements and any limitations of accelerated testing. Use the results to identify routing variables, checkpoints or polling loops that can be simplified without moving the entire orchestration back into Orders or weakening recovery guarantees.

Fable consultation supported examining state-machine complexity, validator scope and unresolved runtime dependencies. This recommendation does not adopt a replacement scheduler, move service-readiness checks into Orders, or treat all consultation suggestions as verified findings. It leaves workflow versioning and business-service ownership unchanged.


## Payment interaction and workflow waiting proposal

**Status — 2026-10-18:** user-authorized integration proposal, informed by Fable consultation. This is a proposed separation of responsibilities, not an implemented Payments API, a new order-state enum or a settled invoice sequence. The reviewed Workflow design consumes a Payments authorization result; the user clarified that payment initiation belongs to Billing and Payments, never Orders Workflow. Their reciprocal integration contract still needs definition.

**Proposed direction.** Orders Lifecycle records the order's need for a financial action, based on evidence from its financial owner, and exposes that need and the relevant request reference through an authorized order read. A notification may announce the change, but refreshing the page or missing a notification must not lose the action. Exact statuses and event schemas remain open. Lifecycle does not calculate prices, infer payment eligibility or acquire ownership of invoices or provider sessions by recording this need.

Billing and Payments own payment initiation and durable recovery. Their financial flow creates or recovers the payment request idempotently and retains its association with the order, order version and financial purpose. Orders Workflow does not create a payment or a checkout session and does not execute the payment process. The UI may invoke the financial flow and open its returned session; it does not own recovery, payment completion or cancellation. The trigger and division of responsibilities within Billing and Payments remain to be specified. A lost create response is recovered by the financial owner under the original request identity, not retried as an unrelated payment.

Payments owns provider interaction, session lifecycle and authoritative financial outcomes. Card entry and any bank challenge belong to the customer/provider interaction outside the workflow definition. Workflow can wait for required customer action and financial evidence, wake on a notification, and reread order financial readiness through Billing when notifications or replies are missing. Billing uses authoritative Payments facts within the financial flow; this does not introduce a second, direct Payments readiness decision in Workflow. A browser redirect or client assertion is not payment proof; Payments validates provider notifications or queries the provider before reporting an authoritative outcome. Closing the browser does not cancel the durable request or erase recovery work.

**Illustrative PaaS checkout.** After the applicable approval and financial prerequisites, the Billing/Payments flow establishes the payment request for the accepted order version. The UI opens its Payments session and shows the outstanding action. The customer completes the provider interaction; Payments records the result, and Workflow consumes the evidence required for its next step. This example does not prescribe whether an invoice precedes payment, whether authorization and capture are separate, or who issues an invoice. Those choices belong to the agreed financial scenario within Billing and Payments.

**Financial and recovery boundaries.**

- Keep authorization, capture and final paid-service admission distinct. An authorized card is not proof that prepayment has been collected. Preserve OW-P02's protection-before-capture ordering and OW-P03's final financial evidence; this proposal does not move capture after service readiness. Any Workflow handoff of order/protection evidence under OW-P02 is not authority for Workflow to create a payment or run collection: the financial flow remains owned by Billing and Payments. Prepayment may precede initial activation, while service accrual still starts at the agreed activation boundary.
- Correlate results with the original order/version and financial request. Results for canceled or superseded work cannot authorize new fulfillment, but must not be discarded: recover whether funds were authorized or captured and retain the required void, refund or reconciliation work under the financial owner's contract.
- A timeout or missing result remains uncertain. Starting a fresh payment attempt requires the Payments retry/settlement contract so an unresolved earlier attempt cannot silently become a duplicate charge. Recover the original request after a lost response or restart.
- Session expiry, provider authorization expiry and commercial commitment expiry are different facts. A new browser session must not silently replace a financial obligation or invalidate a protected purchase. Agree on re-entry, reauthorization and remedy behavior with Payments and the commercial owner rather than unconditionally repricing or canceling on an old quote timestamp.
- Postpay paths use their own authoritative financial evidence and may need no card session. Neither the notification nor the UI flow makes card checkout mandatory for every order. The prepaid balance path is deferred (SEAMS SUB-B4).

**Reuse Pricing billing timing; keep service admission separate.** The existing [Pricing read contract](../../../pricing/docs/design/07-read-contract-events.md) and [Pricing decision D-421](../../../pricing/docs/DECISIONS.md) already expose each resolved plan item's `billing_timing` with its source: the SKU version's value, otherwise the tenant's default timing. Reuse this resolution and its provenance; do not introduce a new plan-level prepay/postpay flag or have Workflow independently resolve tenant defaults. Preserve the resolved conditions with the purchase evidence under the agreed owner contracts rather than silently reinterpreting an accepted order from later settings.

`advance` versus `arrears` describes billing timing. It does not by itself prove that money has been collected, require collection before service begins, or authorize credit. Billing, with Payments evidence where applicable, determines whether a financial prerequisite blocks this purchase under its accepted terms and applicable payer policy. The Workflow-facing contract must distinguish no financial action required before execution, a prerequisite still pending, a satisfied prerequisite, and an authoritative refusal; these are semantic outcomes to agree, not newly implemented API enum names. Workflow consumes the result and follows Lifecycle's order policy; it neither calculates the financial decision nor initiates payment.

For a usage-based PaaS purchase whose accepted terms allow service now and billing for actual usage later, and whose applicable policy adds no financial prerequisite, Workflow proceeds without waiting for a card payment or inventing a separate postpay approval. Usage-based pricing alone does not select this behavior: the accepted settlement terms and applicable policy do. Additional financial review is required only where that policy explicitly calls for it. Conversely, an advance-billed item must not automatically be treated as requiring confirmed prepayment solely because of `billing_timing`.

During implementation, define the financial-admission interface and how per-item terms produce the agreed order-wide result below, including any validity window or required recheck before deferred activation. This section selects reuse of Pricing's existing timing model; it does not introduce a universal postpay permit, a credit-scoring step, or a new plan field. Planned verification covers SKU timing overriding the tenant default, preservation of resolution provenance, usage/arrears without a payment wait when policy permits it, and advance timing not being mistaken for collection evidence. These checks have not been executed.

**Agreed semantic financial-readiness boundary.** For the specific order version, the deployment-selected financial owner (Billing where selected) interprets order financial readiness; Workflow reads it from that owner, never from a raw Payments authorization. That owner interprets the accepted settlement conditions and applicable financial policy, using Payments as the authority for payment facts. The conceptual results are: no prerequisite before execution; the required prerequisite is satisfied; pending or not yet known; and definitive refusal with a reason. The first two permit continuation through the remaining order guards; pending/unknown never means permission. A refusal is handled under Orders Lifecycle policy rather than causing automatic cancellation in Workflow. Billing timing alone does not decide this result, and usage/postpay does not introduce a mandatory approval unless applicable policy requires one.

This answer concerns beginning order fulfillment, not an unconditional permanent right to activate. Preserve Subscriptions' final activation checks and protected purchase terms, and handle later financial outcomes separately. Waiting must support rereading through Billing during ordinary waiting and after restart, as well as notification-driven rereading, under the deadline and recovery policy below. Recovery does not depend on another notification or on a process restart. Following user agreement, concrete APIs, fields, transport and internal financial-component contracts are deferred to implementation; this proposal fixes responsibilities and meaning only. A further Fable consultation supported these boundaries and the distinction between pending, definitive refusal and final activation checks.

**Agreed order-wide admission and scheduled connection semantics.** Retain the reviewed order-wide financial gate: when Billing's required financial outcome for the order is pending, do not start independent fulfillment of a usage-billed line merely because another line is the one awaiting prepayment. Independent starts can be separate orders. Passing the common gate does not promise simultaneous activation of all lines.

The selected future start date means the start of connection/preparation, not a guarantee that service is already usable at that instant. Workflow waits until the scheduled start is due before requesting initial activation; Subscriptions then begins preparation. Do not prepare the service early by predicting provisioning duration. Initial activation and service-period accrual still begin only after readiness and final checks. The [initial activation readiness contract](../features/05-entitlements.md#initial-activation-readiness-contract) is authoritative: the subscription remains draft while its operation prepares access, and Workflow waits for Subscriptions' final applied result. Renewals, resume and active-subscription changes retain their existing contracts. Credit-dependent initial trial profiles whose required access depends on a post-activation grant remain gated until the joint Billing/entitlement contract resolves that dependency; this order proposal does not enable them. This semantic choice does not tighten the reviewed runtime's timer granularity into an exact-time SLA.

For the illustrative arrangement "pay now, connect later", the date is recorded when ordering, Billing/Payments obtains the required prepayment, Workflow passes the common financial gate and waits until the scheduled start. Before payment is confirmed, the order can already contain that date but remains financially pending. Preserve the accepted conditions and funded acquisition protections during the wait. Prepayment in general does not imply immediate payment at checkout: the agreed terms determine when payment is due, and this example creates no universal payment deadline.

**Agreed activation waiting and preparation duration.** Distinguish the timeout of a dispatch call, the duration of Subscriptions' asynchronous activation operation (including required service preparation), and Workflow's deadline for awaiting its outcome. Expiry of a call, polling budget or waiting deadline is not authoritative activation failure or proof that no external effect occurred. Workflow reconciles the same operation through Subscriptions using its retained identity. If the operation is still running, the applicable order policy determines whether to continue waiting, raise a manual task or request cancellation through the existing guarded path. Unavailable status remains unknown. A timeout alone never permits a second activation under a fresh identity. Cancellation must account for any preparation or activation already applied; it is not an implicit consequence of a timed-out read.

Align the reviewed design's dispatch/retry budgets, reconciliation limits, overdue escalation windows, process lifetime and activation SLA with the supported preparation durations. These bounds have different purposes: a timeout for submitting an activation request is not a limit on the entire asynchronous preparation. Preserve existing unresolved-outcome recovery rather than treating every bound as a new failure rule. Numeric values and service-specific policies are implementation work; this proposal neither extends them automatically nor promises unlimited waiting. Planned checks cover preparation still running at the waiting deadline, an unavailable status read, a late applied result, and cancellation racing with readiness. These are proposed checks, not executed tests.

**Payment waiting deadline and late outcomes — proposed contract.**

1. The seller's order policy, retained by Orders Lifecycle for the relevant order version, defines any deadline for waiting for payment and the action on expiry: request order cancellation or escalate for review. Workflow observes that deadline and invokes the selected order action through Lifecycle. This order deadline is distinct from an API timeout, the workflow's technical lifetime ceiling, a provider session expiry and a financial authorization expiry. Its expiry alone does not establish that no money was collected. Exact policy fields and supported actions require owner agreement; no duration default is introduced here.
2. An accepted cancellation decision must be durably made available to the Billing/Payments flow with the original order/version and financial request association. Use a persisted handoff with recovery and evidence of financial-owner acceptance, or an equivalent agreed protocol; a transient notification alone is insufficient. Financial owners determine the appropriate stop, void, refund or reconciliation action from the actual financial outcome. An unknown payment result remains a recovery obligation, not proof of non-payment.
3. Billing and Payments own late financial outcomes even after the order or its Workflow has closed. They retain the request-to-order association, account for delayed or repeated provider confirmations, and durably track the required remedy to settlement or explicit operator handling. A late successful payment must not automatically reactivate a canceled order or disappear because no Workflow instance is listening. The order process need not remain open solely to receive such results once the financial responsibility is durably established; unresolved handoff remains tracked work. Operational order closure and financial settlement are separate outcomes.

**Agreed cancellation before fulfillment.** While financial readiness is pending, the reviewed design keeps the order `approved`; its existing Workflow cancellation seam starts from `in_fulfillment` (slice 04 §4.2). Extend the Lifecycle-owned cancellation path to cover this pre-fulfillment wait. When the configured payment deadline selects cancellation, Workflow requests cancellation of the relevant order/version. Lifecycle atomically arbitrates that request against beginning fulfillment and checks current state, version and authority. If fulfillment has not begun, it records the permitted pre-fulfillment cancellation and initiates a durable handoff to Billing/Payments to stop/reconcile the associated payment. This requires an owner-contract extension, not an assumption that the current Workflow seam already supports it.

If fulfillment has won the race, the request must follow the existing Workflow-mediated cancellation/compensation path with the required authorization; do not report pre-fulfillment cancellation or bypass compensation. Payment confirmation alone does not decide that race or restart a canceled order. Stop/recovery retains the original financial identity and the outcome rules below. Lifecycle also owns reconciliation with its existing order-expiry policy so that deadline processing and expiry cannot create conflicting transitions or duplicate financial work. Concrete operations, delivery mechanism and policy fields remain implementation details.

**Stopping a payment when the order waiting deadline expires.** When the configured expiry action selects cancellation, Workflow requests the appropriate Lifecycle transition and coordinates a durable request to stop the original payment through the Billing/Payments contract. This coordination does not create or execute a payment in Workflow and does not call the provider directly. Repeated stop requests retain the same financial request association and recovery identity. Billing and Payments determine whether the provider operation can still be stopped and report an authoritative outcome:

| Financial stop outcome | Order-process consequence |
| --- | --- |
| Confirmed stopped, with no collection for the original request | The financial-stop leg is settled; Workflow may complete the applicable order-cancellation path once its other required legs are settled. |
| Collection already occurred or won the race with stopping | Do not report the payment as stopped. Billing and Payments establish the required remedy, such as a refund, under the agreed financial policy. Workflow accounts for that outcome; an already canceled order is not automatically restarted. Operational closure may precede final settlement only with durably accepted financial-remedy ownership. |
| Pending or unknown | Keep stop/recovery work outstanding and query the original financial operation. A timeout or acceptance of the stop request is not proof of stopping, non-payment or refund. Do not mark the financial-stop leg complete. |

Expiry alone is not a completed cancellation. If policy chose review rather than cancellation and the order remains eligible for a decision, Workflow consumes a late confirmed payment and follows Lifecycle's authorized decision on continuation. If cancellation has already committed, a late collection belongs to the financial-remedy path. Thus Workflow handles the effect on order execution while Billing and Payments handle the financial effect; neither discards a result because it arrived after the waiting deadline.

Extend the planned checks with a stop accepted before collection; collection winning concurrently with stop; a lost stop response followed by recovery; and repeated stop requests. Verify the authoritative outcome, no duplicate remedy, no automatic resurrection of a canceled order, and no claim of payment cessation based solely on sending a request. These are proposed checks, not executed tests.

Additional planned checks: payment completes just before the order deadline but its confirmation arrives afterward; cancellation delivery fails and is recovered; payment success arrives after Workflow termination; and a late confirmation is delivered repeatedly. Verify that the original financial effect is accounted for once, the canceled order is not restarted, and remedy ownership survives closing or restarting the order process. These checks have not been executed.

**Open owner decisions.** Within the established Billing/Payments ownership, agree the initiation trigger and owner of the cross-service association; the request/session and lookup contracts; action visibility and notification delivery; invoice handling and timing; the relevant financial evidence for each settlement scheme; deadlines and retry/re-entry behavior; and handling of cancellation or late success. An event announcing that payment is required is a candidate integration mechanism, not a substitute for these contracts.

**Planned checks, not executed tests.** Refresh or close the browser during payment; lose a session-create response; miss or duplicate a provider notification; restart Workflow while waiting; receive successful payment after order cancellation or supersession; expire the UI session while a financial outcome remains unknown; and follow postpay paths without opening card checkout. Verify durable request recovery, no duplicate charge, no activation from a browser assertion, and retained financial remedies for late outcomes.

The consultation supported server-owned payment recovery and keeping UI interaction outside workflow execution. Suggestions to discard old-version payment results or move capture universally after service readiness were not adopted. This section changes only the local proposal.
