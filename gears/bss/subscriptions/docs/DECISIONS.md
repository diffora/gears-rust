## Current disposition

Subscriptions owns accepted customer terms, lifecycle decisions, commercial revisions, durable operations and money-free commercial facts. Entitlements owns applying commercial posture to quotas, licenses and resources, including adapter contracts and reconciliation. Pricing stores immutable commercial rules; Rating values accepted facts; Billing owns financial allocation, posting and correction.

Supported changes are immediate quantity increases and component additions. Orders future starts, cancellation schedules, normal renewal/trial timers and guarded early trial conversion remain supported under their capability gates. Separate customer acceptance confirmation, ramps, co-termination, collection pause, notification delivery, tenant/payer transfer, administrative historical correction, import, trial extension/repeat, standalone acquisition, scheduled composition/resume, linked subscription replacement and general base-plan changes are outside the current scope. Unsupported commands refuse before effects, including through operator wrappers.

Missing required notice or acceptance cannot be treated as satisfied; terms that need one are refused at acceptance instead (SUB-D-69). Legacy-system migration is not required.

Operational provenance, immutable history, authorization and recoverable domain events remain required. The local operation audit required by SUB-D-76 is in scope; a cross-gear audit service with query and export is deferred. Current-system replay and Rating/Billing corrections remain required.

## Lifecycle and commercial records

**SUB-D-01: Scheduled lifecycle intents.** Cancellation supports immediate, end-of-term and explicit-date modes. The cancel mode is a typed value of these three: `immediate`, `end_of_term` and `explicit_date`. Only `immediate` applies in this version; the two scheduled modes refuse `capability_closed` (`scheduled_cancel`) before effects, and any other text is an invalid request (SUB-D-105). Pending intents are durable. They can be withdrawn before application and are evaluated by the normal guards at their boundary. Scheduling is planning evidence, not applied service or a billable fact. Scheduled composition and resume are unsupported.

**SUB-D-02: Quantity changes.** Quantity increases and component additions are first-class transitions. One request has one expected revision, one effective instant and one atomic resulting composition. Existing lots retain their terms; the delta receives its own immutable accepted terms and purchase provenance. Decreases and removals are unsupported.

**SUB-D-03: Collection pause scope.** Collection pause is unsupported in the current profile.

**SUB-D-04: Ramp scope.** Contract-authored multi-step ramps are unsupported in the current profile.

**SUB-D-05: Activation dates.** Contract booking and the caller's requested start are distinct from actual service activation. Subscriptions records actual activation T locally after initial readiness and final guards. The subscription is `preparing` during preparation (SUB-D-58). A profile requiring separate customer acceptance cannot launch without that capability.

**SUB-D-06: Immutable ordering identity.** `orderingTenantId` is fixed at creation and supplies the subscription stream/partition identity. Creation-time commercial tenant axes remain explicit; tenant/payer transfer is unsupported.

**SUB-D-07: Commercial and monetary ownership.** Subscriptions publishes money-free recurring, usage-parent and qualifying one-time facts. Rating values them from frozen accepted terms. Billing applies the complete financial result, existing advances and final invoice rounding/corrections. Replay does not create another obligation or collection.

**SUB-D-08: One mutation path.** Every supported mutation, including create, renewal, intent withdrawal and archive, uses the durable operation envelope and local commit path. Creation deduplicates on its business identity across all ordering tenants (SUB-D-94) and starts the revision stream. Unsupported operations cannot enter through alternate administrative endpoints.

**SUB-D-09: Event authority.** Subscriptions owns its lifecycle, intent and commercial-fact events. State, operation outcome and publication intent commit atomically. Provider execution observations do not become a second source of commercial decisions.

**SUB-D-11: Draft cancellation.** A draft can be cancelled through the ordinary guarded operation. An unconfirmed draft has no automatic occupancy claim; its configurable housekeeping TTL has no financial effect. Protected purchase claims and unresolved preparation/payment effects survive draft cleanup and require their own fenced resolution.

**SUB-D-12: Renewal collection.** Collection-pause renewal bypass is unsupported; renewal retains its required financial guards.

**SUB-D-13: Term boundaries.** `autoRenew = false` reaches the normal end-of-term cancellation route. Renewal recovery advances an agreed term occurrence at most once and retains its accepted boundary and billing posture. A payment outcome alone does not resume service or revive a terminal subscription. [Renewal and offboarding](features/04-renewal-retention.md#7-renewal-and-offboarding-integration-contract) defines the applicable late-success and suspension cases.

**SUB-D-15: Manual change scope.** Immediate supported purchases use the ordinary atomic composition path. No ramp schedule or ramp-override operation is enabled.

**SUB-D-16: Nonpayment dwell.** Take the dwell policy from the terms frozen for the originating renewal/collection episode, and snapshot its provenance, start and deadline when that blocking cause commits. A new cause on an already suspended subscription receives its own dwell; existing causes keep their deadlines. Configuration changes do not move that running deadline. The platform default is 90 days. Expiry submits guarded cancellation; only applied cancellation releases the subscription's occupancy claim.

**SUB-D-17: Required notice.** Renewal or continuation must satisfy the notice/assent prerequisites of its accepted terms. A price successor is not permission to amend those terms. Notification delivery is deferred, so terms that require a notice are refused at acceptance (SUB-D-69) instead of blocking an action at runtime.

**SUB-D-18: Cancellation money boundary.** Cancellation updates commercial obligations through immutable successor facts and seals. Subscriptions computes no refund, credit or early-termination amount. Billing handles financial correction and settlement under accepted terms and explicit cutoff evidence.

**SUB-D-19: Per-component facts.** A commercial obligation includes the component/interval identity within its subscription and period. Distinct component obligations cannot collide under one subscription-wide key. Rating may derive multiple calculation windows or lines from one fact.

**SUB-D-20: As-of attribution.** Facts describe the effective composition, quantities, payer, terms and served intervals of their economic time. Processing delay must not substitute current catalog data or current attribution. Complete historical evidence remains recoverable independently of event delivery.

**SUB-D-21: Component and fact identity.** Component intervals retain stable identities. Mid-period additions and terminal boundaries publish the complete affected commercial obligations, including earlier served intervals. Same-obligation quantity changes publish full successor versions; they do not create an independent monetary delta. Trial obligations retain their accepted terms and actual episode boundaries.

**SUB-D-22: Dwell lifecycle.** Authoritative payment resolution clears only the matching blocking cause and its dwell. Other causes retain their timers. Guarded resume requires all blocking suspension causes resolved or discharged; applied terminal cancellation voids their lifecycle timers without erasing financial debt. An authoritative dispute can hold the episode's clock under the selected policy. Retry recovers the same cancellation operation and recorded resource outcome. No pending cancellation, timer or timeout releases a retained place.

**SUB-D-23: Intent firing failures.** Transient dependency uncertainty retries with bounded backoff and retained suppressions. Definitive refusal becomes an explicit failed intent. If a state precondition is expected to clear, such as conversion while suspended, park the intent and pause its firing deadline until the unblocking transition. A terminal lifecycle outcome settles the pending work.

**SUB-D-24: One-time valuation.** A qualifying agreed action produces an amount-less fact valued by Rating from immutable accepted terms. Billing owns financial application. An advance is applied to the same obligation and cannot be collected again at activation.

**SUB-D-25: Reason-aware cancellation.** Cancellation carries its reason, effective cutoff, term and period evidence. Customer/operator early termination can be eligible for contractual treatment; natural term expiry and nonpayment exhaustion do not invent an early-termination fee. Exact financial consequences remain governed by accepted terms and Billing's contract.

**SUB-D-27: Billing calendar.** Subscriptions derives period identity from its frozen `BillingTerms` fields `cycle`, `anchor`, `anchor_at` and `timezone`, as exposed by the Pricing SDK. `anchor` selects `Calendar` or `SubscriptionStart`; `anchor_at` preserves the resolved instant. A month-end clamp does not drift: January 31 reaches February 28/29 and then March 31. Rating uses the same geometry without independently creating a second recurring obligation.

**SUB-D-28: Chargeable-action occurrence.** Each separately agreed chargeable business action has one durable occurrence identity independent of prices and technical attempts. Retry, resume, trial conversion, cancellation/re-add, catalog change and reprovision do not create a fee by themselves. Correcting the original action retains its occurrence and publishes a successor fact/seal. Reserving an ID, prepaying or preparing resources does not prove the action occurred.

**SUB-D-29: PriceBook bindings.** Accepted bindings retain price IDs, revision/item identity, selected dimensions, product evidence and policy/invoice terms. Publishing a price revision does not change existing purchases. Renewal resolves under accepted renewal policy; an in-term binding's own `ends_on` requires explicit continuation authority. `effective_to` alone does not terminate a pin. Replay reads pinned immutable money.

## Ownership and recovery

**SUB-D-30: Commercial posture.** Subscriptions records `CommercialPostureV1` with the source revision and outbox. A lifecycle command records its posture as a posture row in its own commit, and a worker dispatches it after the commit (SUB-D-106). Entitlements applies it through provider-specific capabilities. A local subscription receipt does not prove applied provider access; initial activation consumes the readiness evidence defined by SUB-D-55–57.

**SUB-D-31: Pricing adapter.** The [Pricing integration](DESIGN.md#332-pricing-acceptance-and-accepted-time) uses the read, acceptance and sellability ports. An eligibility observation is not a durable purchase commitment. Accepted commercial evidence is separate from Rating calculation snapshots. Future-purchase and trial extensions follow the [accepted-acquisition](DESIGN.md#accepted-acquisition-evidence) and [trial-evidence](DESIGN.md#336-trial-terms-and-provider-evidence) contracts. The [foundation](features/01-foundation.md#acquisition-recovery) and [trial](features/06-trials.md#credit-preparation-and-recovery) features define recovery.

**SUB-D-32: Commercial fact authority.** Subscriptions owns immutable full fact versions, complete payer-specific commercial membership and exact seals. Usage parents open with the commercial period; Rating owns child-window work. Full-state attribution segments, facts, membership and source checkpoints are recoverable by pull. Commercial completeness, usage-source coverage and Rating calculation completeness are independent. Removal publishes explicit zero-obligation evidence; it does not erase history.

**SUB-D-33: Durable operation recovery.** Acceptance, local state and outbox commit together. Business create identity is `(originType, originId, originVersion, attemptId, lineId)`; for Orders it includes order/version/attempt/line, but not receiver generation and not the ordering tenant, so one origin line binds at most one subscription in the platform (SUB-D-94). Neither a new transport key nor a new generation can create another draft for that identity, and a create recovered under either writes nothing (SUB-D-97). Settle unknown create under the same serialization boundary as create; negative settlement leaves a durable no-create tombstone covering all generations of that attempt. The reservation uses the separate origin/version/line key in its ordering tenant and its own release tombstone (SUB-D-100, SUB-D-101); attempt settlement does not end the reservation. Acceptance is distinct from terminal application. Unknown external effects retain recovery and cannot be discarded by expiry, withdrawal or compensation. Remote calls run outside the local commit lock; application revalidates local fences.

Technical retries retain the original attempt. A successor attempt requires proven predecessor non-application, safe effect settlement/quarantine, financial recovery and exclusive reassociation to the same valid acquisition. Preserve the existing claim, payment/commitment, charge occurrence and predecessor lineage. Applied predecessors are recovered. A changed order version is a commercial amendment, with explicit claim/commitment mapping and financial applicability; it is not a technical retry or automatic cross-payer funding transfer.

**SUB-D-34: Atomic composition.** Validate the entire resulting composition against one expected revision and effective instant, self-exempting the subscription's existing occupancy. Purchase retries create no second lot. Commit composition, lots, operation result, affected full facts, successor seal and outbox atomically. Unsupported multi-date changes refuse. [Changes](features/03-changes.md#apply-one-atomic-changecomposition) defines the procedure.

**SUB-D-35: Resource disposition.** Suspension preserves data-bearing resources. Cancellation separates confirmed charging cutoff, applied commercial cancellation, actual resource stop and deletion. Ordinary customer accrual ends at the confirmed cutoff despite delayed stop, or at an authoritative earlier offboarding stop. Actual runtime remains truthful. Retained data alone creates no fee; separately agreed retention terms may create an obligation.

Cancellation confirmation requires authorization, commercial eligibility and durable cutoff recording. A request receipt is insufficient. Withdrawal/rescheduling requires an explicit versioned confirmed action before cutoff and cannot restore past charges. After cutoff, service requires a valid guarded route. Platform-fault/maintenance stops beyond the accepted SLA end ordinary accrual at authoritative stop; operator/abuse/policy stops follow the accepted suspension billing posture and do not create a cutoff by themselves.

Explicitly bound agreed terms take precedence over versioned configuration, which is the policy set resolved from Settings Service by platform and seller-tenant scope (SUB-D-72). Freeze the selected values and deadlines. The [current platform defaults](PRD.md#platform-defaults-and-contract-binding) are 7-day grace, 90-day nonpayment dwell, 30-day retention and 90-day archive dwell with completed data disposition. Retention notices have 7-day and 1-day lead times; their scheduling and delivery are deferred, and the v1 default retention does not require them, so their absence does not block deletion (SUB-D-69). Hold prevents purge. Final hold release creates a new recorded generation/deadline. A Contract ending mid-term leaves the running term unchanged; next renewal uses an explicitly bound successor or the applicable platform defaults, which carry no notice requirement in this version (SUB-D-69).

Financial evidence retention is separate from resource-data retention.

**SUB-D-36: Trials.** Pricing PlanRevision carries an optional free-period block with explicit zero prices OR a monetary-credit block covering ordinary tariff value. Products owns SKU identity/structure; Billing owns grants, allocations and corrections; Rating retains gross valuation; Subscriptions owns episode boundaries and conversion. Paid conversion follows the accepted settlement scheme: confirmed prescribed prepayment or durable authorization to serve on credit. A card is not universally required. Unknown financial evidence grants no permission.

Definitive prepay failure causes no paid start or waiting-gap debt; normal expiry retains data, and later paid service needs a supported guarded route.

Credit trials require one operation-bound Billing grant commitment before preparation. Successful local T makes that same grant effective; later Billing recording is recovery, not a new access gate. Pre-T preparation consumes no bonus. Eligible residual before component `B` is platform-borne, including the hourly remainder and late positive corrections; residual from `B` follows paid terms regardless of arrival time (SUB-D-67). Original grant expiry stays exact; expiry without conversion creates no paid boundary.

Full Rating corrections adjust the same grant once. Original expiry, restrictions and result lineage remain. After expiry, corrected accounting creates no spendable credit. Correction cannot reopen an exhausted/closed episode or reverse an applied paid conversion. Early guarded conversion preserves eligible unused credit on the same grant until original expiry. Concurrent initial trial acquisition is serialized by payer/plan; served-episode history prevents repeat trials across new subscription IDs. Extension, repeat, regrant, stacking, transfer and reactivation are unsupported.

Free-period delivery does not require a promotional-grant provider; credit delivery does.

**SUB-D-38: Documentation authority.** PRD owns observable requirements; DESIGN owns architecture; features own procedures and acceptance; DECOMPOSITION assigns each active FR/NFR one primary feature; UPSTREAM_REQS records dependency evidence; this register owns decisions. [UPSTREAM_REQS](UPSTREAM_REQS.md#25-provider-prerequisites-and-bilateral-acceptance) distinguishes required provider contracts from owner adoption and measured conformance.

**SUB-D-39: Suspended purchase refusal.** Quantity increases, seat purchases and component additions refuse while suspended at both submission and commit. Perform a separate authorized guarded resume before the normal active change. Purchase does not resume service or queue itself. If suspension wins, refuse the unapplied purchase and recover staged effects; if purchase applied first, retain its obligations and reconcile remaining delivery under the later suspension fence. Draft changes retain their separate S-U01 gate.

Renewal recovery and unrelated administration retain their own contracts.

## Commercial valuation

**SUB-D-40: Exact commercial seals.** Every immutable seal names exact `(fact_id, fact_version, payload_digest)` members. Billing accepts only complete results matching those entries. Changed commercial content/membership needs a successor seal. A newer Rating calculation for the same exact fact may produce a successor financial target under the existing seal. Commercial-head freshness, complete manifests, invoice-version fencing and unknown predecessor recovery still apply.

**SUB-D-41: Authorized continuation.** Automatic in-term continuation at `ends_on` requires explicitly accepted policy and satisfied notice/assent. Pricing selects a permissible successor; Subscriptions authorizes and publishes its full fact and exact seal atomically. Rating cannot substitute terms. Missing authority or covered pricing leaves full valuation pending; the known prefix is not a complete result. No implicit debt, free-service or shutdown policy follows.

**SUB-D-42: Explicit unresolved interval.** An established obligation with a known boundary but no authorized continuation publishes a full successor fact with an explicit `Unresolved` interval and successor exact seal. Keep known intervals and lot/quantity/purchase lineage. Rating advances the source head but leaves full valuation pending; Billing cannot substitute an older result or infer automatic reversal. Resolution creates another full fact/seal. Implicit gaps and unpriced new admission are invalid.

**SUB-D-43: One-time calculation basis.** `Once` means a flat action fee; `Units { quantity, unit }` freezes the positive exact action quantity and agreed unit. Rating refuses missing/incompatible basis instead of assuming one or current subscription quantity. Flat action fees do not multiply by seats; later increases do not change the original action. Distinct additional fees require separately agreed actions.

**SUB-D-44: Structured pending reasons.** Rating's SDK-readable status for an exact pending fact contains a nonempty blocker list: stable code, affected interval/lot/window/dependency, responsible source, evidence and expected resolution route. Subscriptions' unresolved reasons remain source authority; Rating owns its own coverage/recovery findings. Previous complete results identify their own source versions and cannot establish current completion.

**SUB-D-45: Empty, zero and pending.** A complete empty replacement removes only that fact's former monetary contributions through Billing's guarded replacement. An active zero-valued obligation keeps classified zero lines, accepted terms and provenance. Missing conditions/data yields structured pending without complete monetary delivery. Zero usage still needs coverage and applicable minimum valuation; a zero total cannot erase lines or prove refund completion.

**SUB-D-46: Recurring flat and pooled period floor.** Requested of Rating and Pricing under S-U08; the rule and its examples are recorded in [UPSTREAM_REQS §2.3](UPSTREAM_REQS.md#23-rating-and-billing) as `cpt-cf-bss-subscriptions-upreq-valuation-rules` (SUB-D-75).

**SUB-D-47: Per-resource minimum.** Requested of Rating and Pricing under S-U08; the rule and its examples are recorded in [UPSTREAM_REQS §2.3](UPSTREAM_REQS.md#23-rating-and-billing) as `cpt-cf-bss-subscriptions-upreq-valuation-rules` (SUB-D-75).

**SUB-D-48: Usage kind and resource group.** Requested of Rating and Pricing under S-U08; the rule and its examples are recorded in [UPSTREAM_REQS §2.3](UPSTREAM_REQS.md#23-rating-and-billing) as `cpt-cf-bss-subscriptions-upreq-valuation-rules` (SUB-D-75).

## Readiness and execution

**SUB-D-55: Initial readiness before active.** Initial usable access precedes local active/T and service-period start. Subscriptions persists the activation operation and preparation posture before dispatch. Entitlements owns the complete immutable required-assignment/producer manifest and reports an aggregate result bound to that exact operation/preparation revision/digest. An explicit empty manifest is required for a no-preparation profile. Subscriptions validates its binding and all final guards, then commits active state, fresh local T, period start, applied receipt and outbox together.

Required initial assignments include account creation/linking, included deployment and usable permissions; first login and unrelated later resource creation are not prerequisites. Late/unknown results remain recoverable, and no readiness result is not proof of non-execution. License Resolver is read/check only; issuing/feed belongs to the selected execution owner. Renewal, guarded resume, conversion and active modifications retain their separate contracts. See [the consumer readiness protocol](features/05-entitlements.md#initial-activation-readiness-contract).

**SUB-D-56: Entitlements ownership.** Entitlements owns rule semantics, provider-desired state, readiness manifests, adapter contracts and reconciliation. Subscriptions publishes `CommercialPostureV1` atomically with its source revision and consumes `ReadinessObservationV1`. Pricing stores quota/grant rules on PlanRevision and freezes them in acceptance. Adapter requests carry authorized scope, assignment and desired revision, never `subscriptionId`.

Disposition/hold changes publish complete snapshots in newer posture revisions even without a lifecycle status change; receipt-only persistence does not create a new commercial revision. Allocation preparation uses the authorized operation/scope and purchased capacity, counts real occupied resources before T and retains quota/source/member/allocation identities through T. Platform-borne cost does not erase occupancy. Fenced non-activation stops new allocation; occupied capacity is freed only by confirmed resource release and quota settlement. Shared-resource cleanup requires explicit authority.

Checks can carry `purpose = readiness_check` so providers recognize preparation. The provider selects inspection, dry-run or real execution and its accounting; the marker grants no permission, exemption, free quota or success guarantee. No consuming probe or service allowance is mandatory. Real effects still require idempotency, provenance and recovery. Counter/window identity survives activation. The current quota scope remains allocation-only; this recognition hook does not enable deferred consumable-limit enforcement.

Purchased capacity remains the desired commercial cap. An independent provider restriction is an observed discrepancy, not a purchase rewrite. Known incompatible offers cannot be accepted/captured; later discovered incompatibility retains the obligation and remediation. Required initial readiness cannot pass merely because a smaller environment works. Observations have their own ordering; no implicit counter reset, eviction or refund follows.

**SUB-D-57: Held readiness and activation resolution.** Entitlements holds prepared assignments under a durable commitment for the exact activation operation/preparation revision/manifest. Subscriptions serializes activation and ordinary withdrawal on one local fence and publishes the immutable terminal resolution through the posture contract. No remote call runs under that local lock. Timeout, absent/unreachable reads or failed guards do not authorize cleanup.

Both `applied(T)` and `closed_without_activation` identify authenticated namespace/scope, operation, preparation revision/digest and decision version. Applied also binds the complete manifest and commitment. Closure works before a manifest/commitment exists or arrives; it carries known bindings, fences late preparation/readiness and lets Entitlements recover all partial or later-discovered effects of that generation. Old closure cannot affect a successor. Applied continues the same assignments; proven non-activation allows only authorized cleanup.

Independently authorized emergency security blocking remains immediate and reports correlated evidence. An already committed activation remains historical truth and follows subsequent guarded lifecycle/financial policy. Held readiness is not an uptime guarantee. Authorized pre-T and never-activated preparation use is platform-borne, creates no ordinary customer service charge and consumes no trial bonus. Profiles bound exposure and supply durable settlement without changing accepted purchase validity or financial admission.

Each attempt snapshots preparation-policy version, authorized start and deadline; waiting for the agreed future start is separate. Expiry/definitive failure closes that attempt at the activation fence; an earlier applied result retains T. Orders remediates the same purchase/payment/claim, without automatic purchase cancellation/refund. Unknown outcome retains effects. Successor admission requires predecessor non-application and safely settled/quarantined effects under generation fences. Retries, restarts and profile edits cannot extend the deadline or bought start constraints.

Pausing the preparation deadline does not pause elapsed held-resource age. A profile that permits Orders holds requires an owner-agreed exposure bound and recovery action across Orders/Workflow, Subscriptions and Entitlements before enablement (S-U01/S-U04/S-U10). The bound must account for paused time. Reaching it cannot locally expire held readiness, release a paid claim or authorize cleanup: the authorized owner must revoke/settle through the activation fence and recover non-application first. Unknown outcomes retain protection and remain visible for owner recovery; this design does not invent an automatic hold expiry.

**SUB-D-58: `preparing` status.** A subscription is `preparing` from the authorized start of its initial preparation to the guarded activation commit. Subscriptions alone writes this status, in the same transaction that persists or closes the activation operation; no provider or Entitlements observation changes it. Closure without activation (deadline, definitive failure, ordinary withdrawal of the attempt) returns the subscription to `draft` and records the reason in the operation's activation resolution; a successor attempt moves it to `preparing` again. Only an explicit subscription cancellation during preparation moves it to `cancelled`, under the SUB-D-57 fence (SUB-D-105); Orders compensation that withdraws an attempt and then voids the draft passes through `draft`. There is no direct `draft → active` edge.

`preparing` is the only in-flight phase that becomes a status, because it is the only one that holds T, accrual and occupancy conversion (SUB-D-55). Resume and active composition changes keep their status while Entitlements delivers asynchronously. For occupancy a `preparing` subscription counts as a draft; its place is the order reservation. A `draft` with no activation operation has never been prepared; a `draft` with a closed operation was prepared and closed. Replaces the 2026-10-18 rule that kept a preparing subscription in `draft` with its progress visible only through the operation.

**SUB-D-59: Receiver attempt pause.** Each activation attempt is a receiver ledger entry keyed by its acquisition origin `(originType, originId, originVersion, attemptId, generation, lineId)` (SUB-D-71; for Orders: order id, version, attempt, generation, line), unique without the ordering tenant (SUB-D-94), with the immutable grant digest and authority and a state `open | paused | revoked | settled`. Admission, the guarded activation commit and the Workflow commands `pause`, `unpause` and `revoke` serialize under the activation operation's local fence, in the fixed lock order with capacity and subscription rows. `pause` (an Orders hold) blocks both new preparation admission and the activation commit for that generation. If pause wins before admission, `draft → preparing` refuses without preparation effects. If preparation already started, pause does not close it, release held readiness or publish a withdrawal, and the subscription stays `preparing`. The preparation deadline stops while paused and continues from the remaining time at `unpause`. A `pause` that arrives after the activation commit is recorded on the drain roster and the subscription stays `active`: an order hold is not a subscription suspension. `revoke` closes the attempt through the ordinary withdrawal path (`preparing → draft`). An exact same-key command replays; a changed payload refuses; a close received before open records a tombstone for that receiver generation. SUB-D-84 fixes the answers of these commands and their effect on a closed or unknown generation; activation of a paused, closed or unknown generation refuses under SUB-D-85. Attempt-wide create settlement additionally uses the stable create identity of SUB-D-33, and a line holds one fence, so a settle of a second attempt of a line refuses until S-U01 opens (SUB-D-95); neither tombstone releases the separate reservation; a higher generation needs a newly authorized Orders grant, never only a larger number. This same-generation `unpause` protocol is a request pending Orders adoption under S-U01. Orders currently resumes with a successor grant and leaves the old generation paused. Such a grant cannot take over existing preparation or reset its remaining deadline in this profile. The owners must agree on this requested resume protocol or explicitly replace it with a preparation-transfer contract before enabling protected acquisition; AP-C14 is required joint evidence.

**SUB-D-60: Fresh execution check before activation.** Before the guarded activation decision, Subscriptions runs the fresh `check_fulfilment` that Orders D-190 requires for the accepted purchase, outside the local lock, and stages its versioned result. The check validates execution of the accepted purchase; it never reprices or replaces accepted bindings. The [execution-check result mapping](DESIGN.md#execution-check-results) covers expiry, explicit price closure/temporary end, SKU retirement, market change, acceptance mismatch and an activation outside the accepted window. A definitive commercial refusal closes the attempt as `closed_without_activation` with its original typed reason (`preparing → draft`); Orders receives the agreed mapping and remediates the retained purchase under S-U01. Authorization, missing evidence, corruption and transport failures keep their own categories. An unavailable Pricing or unknown result cannot authorize activation: the attempt waits within its saved deadline, and deadline closure retains the dependency cause without inventing commercial expiry. Ordinary successor publication or deprecation for new sales alone is not an execution refusal. A durable AP03 commitment requires a versioned Pricing check that explicitly accepts and validates that commitment; Subscriptions cannot ignore a V1 refusal or extend V1 `hold_until`. Missing provider support or result mapping keeps the profile gated before capture. The staged result is rechecked for freshness in the commit like every other observed dependency version; it is not a reusable admission token.

**SUB-D-61: Change-delta acceptance and purchase origin.** A quantity increase or component addition is a new purchase and needs its own accepted delta evidence from Pricing under the S-U16 contract: keyed by the change order, version and line plus the target subscription and the original revision the request is expressed against, carrying the added quantity or component with bindings pinned at the catalog version current at change-order submit. Its frozen instant is the commit of the applied change, not an activation instant; the contract defines its hold window and expiry. A retry after a definitively unapplied attempt keeps the same acceptance and purchase terms. If the expected revision changes, Pricing must supply immutable revalidation evidence binding that acceptance, its digest, the target subscription and the new expected revision. Revalidation checks that the same delta remains applicable; it neither reprices the purchase nor extends its hold or expiry. Missing or refused revalidation blocks the retry under S-U16. Acquisition acceptance (`NewSaleQuery`, `hold`) is not reused for a delta. Until S-U16 is delivered, order-originated increases and additions stay gated. A direct or operator wrapper makes no purchase in this version: it has no accepted delta to execute and no Orders approval or booking, so an increase or addition through a wrapper refuses before effects; wrappers remain for non-purchase operations. Standalone acquisition stays deferred for the same reason.

**SUB-D-62: Change purchase identity and attempts.** The purchase identity of an increase or addition is the change order, version and line: it maps to one lot, one obligation and one S-U16 acceptance, and its payload digest excludes the expected revision. An execution attempt is that identity plus the Orders attempt id and the expected aggregate revision. An exact repeat of an attempt replays its outcome; a changed purchase payload conflicts. A new attempt is admitted only after its predecessor is definitively unapplied (refused, or closed without application); an unknown predecessor outcome is recovered first. The new attempt carries a new expected revision and the same purchase identity, reuses the same delta acceptance with S-U16 revalidation for its new revision, and applies at most once; a delayed predecessor is fenced by its attempt id and cannot apply after a successor. S-U01 association and settlement apply only to the draft-change profile, not to active-subscription changes.

**SUB-D-63: Occupancy claim set.** The occupancy key is `(resourceTenantId, overlapScopeKey, declared product dimensions)`: the resource tenant, the catalog subscription product key and the dimensions that the product declares. It is the same key that Orders uses ([commercial model §3.11](../../../../docs/arch/bss-commercial-model/DESIGN.md#311-read-consistency-projections-and-migration)). The payer is part of the key only when the product declares it as a dimension, never by default. So one sponsor may fund one product for two resources, two funders cannot hold one product for one resource, and a change of payer moves no claim. A subscription holds one claim per distinct occupancy key that its resulting composition maps to: its claim set. Confirmation reserves, and activation converts, every key of the accepted composition. A component addition atomically claims the new keys of the resulting composition without releasing any existing key; a conflict on any new key refuses the whole change and preserves the old composition and claim set. A key leaves the set only when its component leaves the composition, which is deferred, so in this version the set only grows. Suspend retains, resume converts and applied cancel releases the whole set; a lowered limit never evicts a claim. The order-reserved places are the order-line reservations of SUB-D-100; the line's activation converts them without competing (SUB-D-102). The occupancy read returns versioned claim/count/limit evidence (SUB-D-89). Orders sums active, retained-suspended and order-reserved occupancy on every resulting key, counting each protected right once and reusing its own existing claims. Another holder alone is not a conflict: with limit 3 and occupied 1, an addition requiring one new place may proceed. Only insufficient capacity refuses; final admission remains atomic across the claim set. Migration of an existing component's key stays gated under S-Q02/S-U10. The key changes together with Orders, as the occupancy rollout of the commercial model states: a collision report of the stored and the new key comes first, both admission paths switch together under one gate, and stored historical keys are not rewritten. Until that rollout, the stored key is `(payerTenantId, resourceTenantId, overlapScopeKey)` and so includes the payer tenant: a known gap, gated by S-U21 with S-U10. This version adds no migration of the key. Replaces the single-place wording and the claim move for additions.

**SUB-D-64: Cancellation guard on purchases.** A seat purchase, quantity increase or component addition refuses before effects while the target subscription has a pending or unresolved failed cancellation intent of any kind (immediate, end-of-term or future-date), and after a confirmed charging cutoff, whether that cutoff has passed or is scheduled. The guard is checked at submission and again in the completing transaction, next to the status guard. A customer who wants to buy more on a subscription with a scheduled cancellation first withdraws (unschedules) that intent; a purchase that ends at a future cutoff is not supported in this version. The matrix in feature 03 keys on status and on this guard; status alone is not admission.

**SUB-D-65: Conversion lead time.** A scheduled prepay conversion starts its prescribed collection at `E − conversionLeadTime`, where E is the original trial expiry and the lead time is part of the accepted trial terms on the PlanRevision (S-U12). Collection must reach a definitive outcome, applied or failed, before E. If the outcome is still unknown at E, the episode ends at E exactly as a normal expiry: access is revoked, no paid period starts and no debt arises. A capture that succeeds after E does not revive the closed episode (FA-C08); it is remediated as an erroneous collection by refund, or, only with the customer's explicit assent, turned into a new purchase through the ordinary route. Postpay conversion has no collection and is not affected; it needs its authorization before E under the same rule. Pending is therefore never treated as absence of a route and never extends the trial.

**Scheduled conversion collection (proposed S-U07 contract).** Subscriptions owns the due timer at `E − conversionLeadTime` and durably writes one collection request to its outbox for the conversion operation and original episode. The selected financial owner establishes the Billing obligation and directs Payments; Subscriptions does not call a new assumed Payments method. Early and exhaustion-triggered prepay use the same request/recovery contract and stable paid-conversion occurrence; competing triggers cannot start separate collections. Delivery and recovery retain authorized seller/payer/resource scope, the selected financial owner, operation/episode and obligation identity, accepted terms and exclusive expiry E. Before enabling scheduled prepay conversion, owners must agree the exact request/read protocol and a capture-start fence against E and episode closure, including delayed outbox requests. At expiry or earlier terminal closure, Subscriptions durably requests cancellation/void of unstarted collection and unused authorization. The financial owner rejects a delayed capture start for the closed episode. An already-started or unknown capture remains recoverable under its original identity; a late success follows FA-C08 and never reopens the trial. Cancellation acknowledgement alone is not proof that no capture happened. This is an enablement requirement, not a claim that the current SDK provides it.

**SUB-D-66: Credit exhaustion action.** The accepted trial terms of a credit trial carry `exhaustionAction ∈ {convert, end}` (S-U12). With `convert`, authoritative exhaustion is the third conversion trigger, under the same single-episode guard as early and scheduled conversion: it converts at the exhaustion commit when a paid route is authorized (postpay authority, or a prescribed prepay payment already satisfied); for an accepted prepay route without satisfied payment, revoke trial access and enter `conversion-pending` while starting or recovering one collection. This is a trial-episode state, not a seventh subscription status. It preserves data but grants no service or new credit. Its commit advances the aggregate revision and records the restricted posture, waiting interval and outbox together. Complete posture snapshots and guarded resume preserve the restriction; new composition purchases refuse at submission and commit. Reads expose the episode, waiting reason and requested versus observed access revocation. Fact derivation records the commercial wait as unserved while retaining actual stopping-delay usage and platform liability; active status cannot imply served time. Conversion may commit only before original expiry and after all guards pass. Definitive payment failure or original expiry closes the pending conversion as `end` and replaces the waiting restriction with the normal revoked posture, never an unrestricted target; an unknown payment stays recoverable without delaying that closure. A later capture cannot reopen the episode and follows the SUB-D-65 financial remedy or new-purchase route. With `end`, or when no paid route is authorized, exhaustion takes the normal trial end action: access is revoked, data is retained, no debt arises. Exhaustion grants no permission to continue service at platform cost. Actual stopping-delay usage and SUB-D-67 hourly allocation remain platform-borne without granting access or creating customer debt. A missing `exhaustionAction` refuses the credit trial terms.

**SUB-D-67: Hourly-aligned trial boundaries.** A valid early, scheduled or exhaustion conversion records the actual paid transition `T_paid`. Status and access change at that instant. Each component also records its paid rating/allocation boundary `B`: `T_paid` for non-hourly components, or the earliest UTC-hour boundary at or after `T_paid` for hourly-windowed components. At an exact hour `B = T_paid`; no extra free hour is added. Rating and Billing use the same component boundary, not event arrival time.

During the hourly interval `[T_paid, B)`, a free-period component keeps its zero binding. A credit-trial component keeps its previous tariff: eligible expenses may use the same bounded, applicable grant only for service before its original exclusive expiry `E`; the platform bears the remaining value. Exhausted or expired credit covers nothing, and the hourly remainder creates no customer debt. From `B`, accepted paid terms apply after any still-valid eligible bonus coverage. Non-hourly components use paid terms from `T_paid`.

Expiry without a valid paid conversion is not an aligned paid transition: access revocation starts at `E`, and no paid boundary or successor binding is invented. Actual stopping-delay usage after `E` remains platform-borne until access stops, uses no trial credit and is not a trial expense. Rounding never extends access, the trial or grant expiry. Trial start retains the ordinary activation rule for hourly lines.

Facts retain `T_paid` when present, each component's `B`, original `E`, actual served extents and the frozen rule version. Hourly tariff slices change at `B`; actual service and eligibility boundaries remain exact. Do not split a scalar hourly peak at `T_paid` or prorate it by minutes. If grant eligibility or stopping-delay allocation needs evidence the provider cannot supply, keep the affected allocation pending under S-U08/S-U15; never invent coverage, customer debt or an expiry extension.

**SUB-D-68: Two grace episodes.** Grace has two kinds with one evaluated ladder (grace, then suspension with nonpayment dwell, then cancellation). A **renewal grace** covers a term that never started: a definitive financial refusal at renewal anchors it at the accepted term boundary, the next-term obligation stays blocked, and a late success completes the same renewal with the term starting at its boundary. A **collection grace** covers a term already renewed or applied whose invoice is due and unpaid: Billing's authoritative due/unpaid outcome anchors it at the due/unpaid effective time and binds it to `(subscriptionId, stable Billing collection-obligation ID)` in authorized payer/tenant scope. The applied term is provenance, not the collection episode key. One renewal grace exists per term occurrence; one collection grace exists per distinct collection obligation, so one term may have several collection episodes. Invoice/fact references and their corrected versions remain ordered evidence on that episode, not new episode identities. Corrections, duplicate failures and retry-exhaustion signals do not restart grace or reset the frozen anchor/deadline. The term is not changed, and resolution of one obligation affects only its own collection episode. Exact obligation identity, correction lineage and outcome ordering require Billing adoption under S-U07. An open collection grace or collection-driven suspension does not by itself disable automatic renewal. The next boundary uses its accepted renewal election and settlement scheme, including prescribed prepay payment or authorized postpay terms. Success retains any suspension and its billing posture; refusal creates the renewal grace, whose exhaustion attaches an independent blocking cause/dwell even if already suspended. Each cause retains its own identity and timeline; resolving one never clears another. Resume requires all blocking suspension causes resolved/discharged and a current term. Terminal subscriptions record later financial outcomes without starting a new lifecycle ladder. The [RO03 contract](features/04-renewal-retention.md#7-renewal-and-offboarding-integration-contract) and RO-C22–RO-C24 define joint evidence.

**SUB-D-69: Notice-dependent terms are an admission gate.** Because notification delivery is deferred, no action in this version may depend on a notice at runtime. Acceptance refuses a binding or Contract whose terms require a notice or assent for renewal, continuation or deletion; the renewal route to the versioned platform defaults carries no price-change notice requirement in this version, so a Contract that ends without a successor renews to the defaults without blocking. Acceptance also refuses a binding with a known in-term `ends_on` unless Pricing supplies continuation evidence (a successor binding or an explicit notice-free continuation rule). An `Unresolved` successor fact therefore arises only from a late-discovered boundary and raises an operator-visible finding with its age. Cancellation bounds further service but leaves already served unresolved intervals and their seals pending. Historical resolution requires recovery of authoritative continuation evidence valid for the served interval, followed by a full fact/seal replacement. Neither an operator nor a new purchase may invent historical prices or mark the missing interval free. S-U08/S-U13 must provide an owner-agreed recovery or financial remedy for missing historical authority before this profile is enabled; there is no local `confirmAcceptance` or waiver shortcut. The v1 default retention carries no required notice: the 7-day and 1-day retention notices are deferred scheduling, and their absence does not block deletion. Refines SUB-D-17.

**SUB-D-70: Local no-preparation profile.** A deployment may select the service profile `no_preparation` for subscriptions that need no provider preparation before service. Under it, Subscriptions itself records the explicit empty manifest and its aggregate `ready` observation in the same transaction that persists the authorized activation operation, with the profile version as evidence; no `CommercialPostureV1` is dispatched for preparation and Entitlements is not called. The subscription passes through `preparing` within that transaction and the guarded activation commit follows at once under the same final guards (SUB-D-55 unchanged otherwise). Lifecycle posture after activation still publishes `CommercialPostureV1` when the deployment runs Entitlements; a deployment without Entitlements enables only profiles that need no provider application at all. Every other profile requires Entitlements and its explicit manifest, empty or not.

**SUB-D-71: Acquisition origin.** A subscription is created from an acquisition origin, not from an order as such. `AcquisitionOriginV1` carries the origin type (a GTS instance), the origin identity and version, the attempt id, the generation and the line id; each operation uses the appropriate subset of these fields. The receiver ledger (SUB-D-59) includes generation. Create and its no-create tombstone use origin type/id/version, attempt and line, excluding generation (SUB-D-33). Neither the ledger key nor the create key includes the ordering tenant (SUB-D-94). The confirmation reservation and its release tombstone use origin type/id/version and line, excluding both attempt and generation, within the ordering tenant (SUB-D-100). Change-purchase identity likewise stays stable across attempts as defined by SUB-D-62. Replay and successor recovery must preserve these distinct scopes; the full envelope is not one shared business key. Orders is the first origin provider: its `originType` is the GTS instance `gts.cf.bss.subscriptions.acquisition_origin.v1~cf.bss.subscriptions.orders_origin.v1` (SUB-D-78), with order id, order version, attempt, generation and line in those fields. An origin provider owns the accepted purchase evidence (S-U01 commitment, S-U16 for changes), the occupancy reservation at confirmation, capture ordering and compensation, the hold, unpause and revoke commands and the remediation of a closed attempt; another origin type (marketplace, checkout, storefront, external CPQ or ERP) is enabled only with an agreed adapter contract that supplies these and executed conformance evidence. In this version only the Orders origin is enabled; a create from an unregistered or disabled origin refuses before effects, and enabling an origin later changes no schema.

**SUB-D-72: Policy set in Settings Service.** The lifecycle policy defaults form one GTS-typed policy set, `gts.cf.bss.subscriptions.policy_set.v1~`: grace, nonpayment dwell, retention, archive dwell, hold-release policy, the activation profile that a seller tenant sets for a resource tenant (service profile, preparation duration and `before_apply` hook budget, SUB-D-85), abandoned-draft TTL, the occupancy `maxConcurrentActive` and scope-key rule, and the approved bounds for order overrides. Subscriptions contributes the setting key `gts.cf.core.settings.setting_type.v1~cf.bss.subscriptions.lifecycle_policy.v1~` in category `subscriptions`, with the separately registered `value_type_id = gts.cf.bss.subscriptions.policy_set.v1~`. Settings Service stores its values, authorizes changes and resolves the effective value through its tenant scope hierarchy; Subscriptions reads it through `SettingsClientV1` and freezes the resolved value with its version and provenance at evaluation, as every frozen deadline already requires. Precedence is platform, then seller tenant (Settings Service scopes), then plan (an override carried by the PlanRevision, Pricing), then Contract (S-U06), then order values within the approved bounds. A legal or governance minimum is a constraint that the owning governance service supplies to Settings Service; Subscriptions refuses an effective value below it. In this version platform and seller-tenant scopes are live through Settings Service (S-U17); plan and Contract overrides stay behind their gates. The platform values of this design (7, 90, 30 and 90 days) are the initial platform-scope settings, not literals in code.

Archive moves `cancelled → archived` under this policy set. At the request, before it judges the status, it reads the archive dwell and the policy version that the subscription's seller tenant sets for its resource tenant. None configured refuses `capability_closed` (`lifecycle_settings`). A dwell outside 1 second to 3,650 days, or a version that is not 1 to 128 visible ASCII characters, reads as unreadable settings and answers `dependency_unavailable`. Neither writes anything. An archive before the cancel instant plus the dwell refuses `invalid_state` (`archive_dwell`). A subscription that was served (it has T) also needs its retention disposition: none configured refuses `capability_closed` (`retention_disposition`), and a disposition that is not complete refuses `invalid_state` (`retention_pending`). Both `invalid_state` refusals are stored. A subscription never served has no disposition to ask. The archive's receipt keeps the policy version. Archive does not wait for an open effect of the subscription's closed activation: its withdrawal or compensation goes on by itself (SUB-D-88). The archive is a lifecycle command (SUB-D-103).

**SUB-D-73: Extension points.** Three vendor extension points are part of the contract surface. (1) Cancellation reasons are well-known instances of `gts.cf.bss.subscriptions.cancel_reason.v1~`; each carries the required traits `financial_class ∈ {early_termination_eligible, no_fee, correction}` and `initiator ∈ {customer, platform}`, beside its `display_name`. The six reasons of this design are platform instances, and a vendor registers its own (fraud, compliance, reseller-initiated, migration) with the same traits; every rule that depends on a reason reads its traits, never its name. A caller may send the platform reasons `customer`, `operator`, `order_compensation` and `saga_superseded`. `term_expired` and `nonpayment_exhausted` come only from the gear's own triggers, so a caller that sends one is refused as an invalid request. A vendor reason refuses `capability_closed` (`vendor_cancel_reason`) until the gear reads the types registry for it (SUB-D-105). (2) Lifecycle hooks expose two points for supported operations: `before_apply` runs after preliminary guards, outside the transaction and before acquiring commit locks, on an immutable versioned snapshot, may only allow or veto with a typed reason, has a time budget from the operation profile, performs no write and no financial or resource effect, and fails closed (timeout or error prevents application as `dependency_unavailable`, with initial-activation recovery as defined below); the completing transaction rechecks the snapshot revision, evidence and hook/profile versions, and stale results require a fresh evaluation outside locks; `after_applied` is delivered from the outbox at least once with the idempotency key `(operationId, revision)`, up to a bounded number of attempts (SUB-D-98), and cannot change the outcome. A hook is a plugin instance of `gts.cf.toolkit.plugins.plugin.v1~cf.bss.subscriptions.lifecycle_hook.v1~` that declares its operations and point and runs on the platform serverless runtime or task engine. The engine carries the hook stage and the identifiers from the first increment; executing hooks is delivered behind gate S-U18, until which the registry is empty and the stage is a no-op. (3) The aggregate has a typed `attributes` slot of a vendor-derived type under `gts.cf.bss.subscriptions.attributes.v1~`, versioned with the revision, readable on every event and read, and never consulted by a commercial rule; it cannot carry or rebind tenant axes. The trial union and the suspend/resume checks stay closed: they are commercial semantics with money and gates (S-U12, S-U07), not vocabularies.

Mandatory internal expiry, exhaustion-end, compensation and attempt closure are observer-only: authenticated internal triggers skip `before_apply` and deliver `after_applied` after commit. A caller-supplied reason cannot select this path; validate operation provenance and authority. For initial activation, a definitive veto closes the attempt through the non-application fence and retains the purchase for remediation. A hook timeout/error prevents activation, records `dependency_unavailable` and remains recoverable on that same attempt within its saved deadline; deadline closure uses the same fence. Neither result automatically refunds the purchase or releases held resources.

**SUB-D-74: Adapter plugins and resolved profiles.** The financial-admission, historical-inventory, usage-coverage and lifecycle-hook seams are ToolKit plugins: a GTS plugin specification registered by this gear, a public and a plugin trait in the SDK, one GTS instance per adapter with a scoped ClientHub client, and resolution through the types registry. Each plugin trait exposes `capabilities()`, read at startup and on configuration change. Selection is by vendor in gear configuration, with a per-seller-tenant override through the policy set for financial admission and usage coverage. A profile is resolved for one seller-tenant scope from its selected instances, effective policy and closed gates, recorded in `ProfileResolutionV1` with scope, policy provenance and selected owner identities. Settings effective-value changes invalidate or recompute affected resolutions before new admissions. Unselected installed instances contribute no capability. Admitted operations retain their original resolution, policy and selected owners for effects and recovery; a later edit cannot silently reroute them. Missing original recovery support leaves the operation pending/unavailable until its agreed recovery or migration protocol resolves it. A capability without its selected instance, declaration or closed gate refuses new work as `capability_closed`.

**SUB-D-75: Owner rules are requests, not decisions.** Valuation rules (recurring flat, minimum fees, windows, partial hours and periods: the former bodies of SUB-D-46 to SUB-D-54) and the trial-credit accounting rules (grant issuance, allocation, same-grant correction, retention after early conversion, platform-borne excess) belong to Rating, Pricing and Billing. This design keeps only what Subscriptions publishes and consumes (fact shape, term slices, lot allocation, evidence references, the Rating result contract) and records the expected owner behaviour as requested requirements in UPSTREAM_REQS §2.3 (`upreq-valuation-rules` under S-U08, `upreq-trial-credit-accounting` under S-U15), with their examples. A request becomes binding when the owner ratifies it in its own register; until then the affected profile stays gated. The texts are offered to Rating, Pricing and the future Billing register through owner pull requests.

**SUB-D-76: Audit record.** The answer to who changed a subscription, when and why does not depend on the event broker. Every applied or refused operation writes one audit record, `gts.cf.bss.subscriptions.audit.v1~`, in the same transaction as its operation record and revision snapshot: subscription, revision before and after, operation identity and kind, asserting principal and delegation evidence (SUB-D-112), reason instance where the operation carries one (a cancel's reason, SUB-D-105), correlation, status before and after, T where applied, outcome and refusal reason, and the refusal detail where the refusal has one (for a hook veto: the hook instance and its reason, SUB-D-86; for a lifecycle command's `invalid_state`: the refused status, `archive_dwell` or `retention_pending`, SUB-D-103; for `acceptance_missing` of a create or an activation: that detail; for a create's `acceptance_mismatch`: the part of the receipt that differs, SUB-D-108). An activation that closes while a preparation effect is still out writes its audit record in the commit that fails its operation, not in the closure commit (SUB-D-81). A refusal that stores nothing (SUB-D-82) and a command that lost every race (SUB-D-99) persist no operation and therefore write no audit record. A recovery is not an operation: a create recovered under a new transport key, a reserve recovered under a new key and a `settle` whose attempt is already final write no audit record (SUB-D-97). It is a projection over records this design already keeps, not a second copy of state. An authorized read `audit_since` returns it by subscription or ordering tenant under the scope, cursor and continuation rules of the recovery reads, and its retention follows the financially referenced history. Domain events remain the feed for external consumers; a platform audit consumer, when one exists, reads `audit_since`. A cross-gear audit subsystem with query and export stays deferred.

**SUB-D-77: REST projection.** The REST surface is a projection of the same `OperationBuilder` operations the SDK exposes under `/subscriptions/v1`: list and read with OData filtering and the revision as `ETag`, the composition reads at an instant and over a range (SUB-D-110), audit reads, operation status, one action endpoint per lifecycle operation, and the recovery reads of feature 09. Create, the receiver ledger commands, the order-line reservation commands and the exact attempt and reservation lookups are SDK methods that the public router does not mount (SUB-D-127). Existing-subscription mutations carry `If-Match` with the expected revision (SUB-D-80). Create and receiver commands instead carry the full authorized acquisition-origin tuple and serialize under its durable receiver fence; they require no nonexistent aggregate ETag. A reservation command carries the order line and serializes on its line lock (SUB-D-90, SUB-D-100). Receiver pause/unpause/revoke/settle, the reservation commands and the exact lookups work before creation and name the ordering tenant explicitly (SUB-D-83). All mutations require an `Idempotency-Key`; an applied action or recovered result returns 200, and pending work 202 with a status path (SUB-D-81). A lifecycle command is always resolved in its request (SUB-D-103). REST member names are snake_case (SUB-D-78). Errors are RFC 9457 problems from the §4.2 registry (SUB-D-79). The OpenAPI document is produced with the code from this resource model, so REST and SDK cannot drift.

**SUB-D-78: Wire names and typed values.** The `originType` value of the Orders origin is the full GTS instance identifier `gts.cf.bss.subscriptions.acquisition_origin.v1~cf.bss.subscriptions.orders_origin.v1`; another origin type is another instance of the same GTS type. Member names in this design are the SDK names. SDK models and event payloads use camelCase (`orderingTenantId`, `originType`, `originId`, `originVersion`, `attemptId`, `generation`, `lineId`). REST bodies, query parameters and answers follow the platform REST rule and use snake_case (`ordering_tenant_id`, `origin_type`, `origin_id`, `origin_version`, `attempt_id`, `generation`, `line_id`). Stored columns use snake_case. GTS type schemas and their instances use snake_case member names, as the types registry stores them (a cancel reason's `financial_class` and `display_name`, SUB-D-73), and the SDK type of a GTS schema keeps those names: the camelCase rule of SDK models does not apply to a GTS payload. A closed set of values is typed on the wire: the activation progress `reason`, `cause` and `ordersMapping` (SUB-D-86), and the component kind, purchase kind, slice service, binding state, unresolved reason and charge kind of the composition reads (SUB-D-110), are open V1 enums in the SDK, so a value of a later version reads as unknown, and typed enums in the REST schema. The REST `orders_mapping` member is text whose OpenAPI schema lists the allowed Orders codes, because the platform REST rule does not allow enum values with hyphens. The text of every value is the same in the SDK, in REST and in storage.

**SUB-D-79: Refusals identified by their category.** The platform's canonical errors carry no reason code for the categories `AlreadyExists`, `Unimplemented`, `ResourceExhausted` and `ServiceUnavailable`. In the [refusal registry](DESIGN.md#refusal-reasons) each of these categories has exactly one reason: `idempotency_mismatch`, `capability_closed`, `occupancy_full` and `dependency_unavailable`. So the category identifies the reason on the wire. The problem detail names what is affected: the reused key, the closed capability, or the full occupancy key, sent as one quota violation per key part (`payer_tenant_id:<id>`, `resource_tenant_id:<id>`, `overlap_scope_key:<key>`), each described `OCCUPANCY_FULL`. The business detail of a `business_conflict` (for example `attempt_settled`, `payload_changed` or `origin_line_bound`) is in the problem detail and in the stored receipt `reason`. Every stored receipt `reason` is lowercase: such a business detail, the registered reason of any other stored refusal (for example `origin_disabled`, or `capability_closed`, whose detail names the closed capability), the closure reason of an activation (SUB-D-86), or the reason of an applied settle (SUB-D-84) or release (SUB-D-101). An uppercase `error_code` is never stored as a reason. The [stored receipt reasons](DESIGN.md#stored-receipt-reasons) in force are listed beside the registry. A second registry reason in one of these four categories is not allowed until the platform can carry its code. The HTTP status of a refusal is its category's SDK mapping, so the split between 409 and 400 follows the category: a refusal by other work in flight is `Aborted` and answers 409 (`business_conflict`, `revision_conflict`, `concurrent_modification`), and a refusal by the source state or a precondition is `FailedPrecondition` and answers 400 (`invalid_state`, `origin_disabled`, `hook_vetoed`). Three of these statuses depart from the platform [REST guideline](../../../../guidelines/DNA/REST/STATUS_CODES.md): a `FailedPrecondition` refusal answers 400 where the guideline gives 409 to a conflict with the resource's state; `capability_closed` (`Unimplemented`) answers 501, a status that the guideline does not cover; and `occupancy_full` (`ResourceExhausted`) answers 429 without the `Retry-After` header that the guideline requires on every 429 ([§10](../../../../guidelines/DNA/REST/API.md#10-rate-limiting--quotas)).

**SUB-D-80: Revision precondition.** A command on an existing subscription carries `If-Match` with the quoted aggregate revision it expects. A missing `If-Match` is an invalid request (400) that names the header, and nothing is written; the platform [REST guideline](../../../../guidelines/DNA/REST/STATUS_CODES.md#client-errors) answers a missing required precondition with 428 (Precondition Required), which the gear does not use. The revision is compared after the replay lookup. A different revision refuses `revision_conflict` (409, category `Aborted`), as the registry maps it; the guideline answers an `If-Match` mismatch with 412 ([§8](../../../../guidelines/DNA/REST/API.md#8-concurrency--idempotency)), which the gear does not use either. That refusal is stored as the operation's failed receipt with its audit record, so a replay of the same key returns the same refusal.

**SUB-D-81: Operation states and the activation answer.** An operation that applies in the transaction that persists it (create, settle, the receiver commands, the lifecycle commands and the reservation commands) is stored `applied`, or `failed` when it is refused after authorization. Activation is stored `approved` by its admission transaction: its permission is staged, and it is not applied yet. It stays `approved` until it resolves. The activation commit resolves it `applied`. A closure without activation, whatever its reason, resolves it `failed` with the closure reason only when no preparation effect of the activation is still out (SUB-D-88). While an effect is neither `withdrawn` nor `confirmed`, the receipt stays `approved`: the operation read shows no operation reason and the closure, with its reason, in the activation progress. The commit that withdraws or confirms the last effect then writes the receipt `failed` with the closure reason and its audit record, exactly once. A closure with no effect out fails the operation in its own commit. A late ready after the failure reopens only the effect's compensation and `pendingCleanup` (SUB-D-84, SUB-D-88); the operation does not move again. These operations do not use `pending`. After admission, the request drives a `no_preparation` activation once within a bounded request budget (SUB-D-87); an `entitlements` activation is left to the driver. The answer is 200 when the operation is resolved by the time the request answers, either `applied` or `failed` after a closure with its reason; the answer carries the subscription, the operation and an `applied` flag that tells the two apart. A closure is an answer, not an error. Otherwise the answer is 202 with the `approved` operation and `Location: /subscriptions/v1/operations/{operationId}`: while the activation prepares, and after a closure while its preparation effect is still out. Work that is still pending is never a refusal, and the registry has no reason for it. Orders Workflow therefore reads an activation's closure from the activation progress (`phase = closed`, with its reason) and its cleanup from `pendingCleanup`, not from a terminal operation state (S-U01).

**SUB-D-82: Refusals that store nothing.** A refusal that can clear without a new request persists nothing: no operation receipt, no audit record and no revision. It is logged, as an unavailable dependency is. A retry with the same `Idempotency-Key` and body is therefore judged again from the start, and is admitted once the condition has cleared. At activation admission these are: a claim-set key is full (`occupancy_full`), the receiver generation is paused (`invalid_state`, `generation_paused`), a preparation effect of the subscription is still in flight (`business_conflict`, `effect_in_flight`), and another origin version of the line prepares (`business_conflict`, `activation_in_progress`, SUB-D-102). At a reserve they are a full key (`occupancy_full`) and a subscription of the line that prepares without a reservation (`activation_in_progress`, SUB-D-100). At a release they are a preparing activation of the line (`activation_in_progress`) and a capture that is not resolved (`business_conflict`, `capture_unresolved`, SUB-D-101). A closed capability also stores nothing, wherever the command finds it: in its request, its configuration, an owner's answer or its transaction. At activation admission this covers `key_movement` and `version_amendment` (SUB-D-102) as well as the closed profile gates of SUB-D-85 and SUB-D-89. The two successor refusals are the exception: `successor_attempt` (SUB-D-95) and `successor_activation` (SUB-D-85) are decided by the stored records, are final for their key and are stored. Every other refusal after authorization is stored as the operation's failed receipt with its audit record and replays (SUB-D-76, SUB-D-80), except an unavailable dependency and a command that lost every race (SUB-D-99), which store nothing either. Storing a refusal departs from the platform REST guideline, which caches no error answer under an `Idempotency-Key` and judges its retry again ([§8](../../../../guidelines/DNA/REST/API.md#8-concurrency--idempotency)): here a stored refusal is final for its key.

**SUB-D-83: Ordering tenant and the caller's reach.** Create, activation, the lifecycle commands on an existing subscription (`suspend`, `resume`, `cancel`, `archive`), the receiver commands (`settle`, `pause`, `unpause`, `revoke`), the order-line reservations (`reserve`, `release`) and the exact attempt and reservation lookups name the ordering tenant explicitly, as `orderingTenantId` in the body or the query. It is never taken from the caller's security context, whose tenant may be the platform root for the Orders service identity. Authorization asks the policy decision point about that tenant only: the caller's grant must reach it. Subscriptions then checks the tenant in Tenant Resolver: it must be active (teardown excepted, below), and, under the interim of SUB-D-125, reachable from the anchor tenant of the grant without crossing a self-managed tenant barrier. A tenant equal to the anchor passes. The point question asks about the ordering tenant alone, in the root-only tenant mode, and its answer passes the returned-scope guard of SUB-D-125 before the gear uses it. The platform's decision point answers a root-only question with the anchor tenant of the grant that reaches the asked tenant (`owner_tenant_id IN [anchor]`), not with the asked tenant, so that answer is fenced only to another tenant. Until S-U20 is met, such an answer takes the interim of SUB-D-125, a known gap: the decision point has judged the grant at the ordering tenant, so the allow is for that tenant; Tenant Resolver only narrows it; the gear builds the command scope `owner_tenant_id IN [ordering tenant]` from that allow, and the anchor is kept as the scope proof (SUB-D-112). The barrier check of the interim relies on that answer: a decision point that echoes the asked tenant instead, as `tr-authz-plugin` and `static-authz-plugin` do, makes every anchor the ordering tenant itself, and the check then passes without a walk. Under the interim, a service caller whose ordering, payer or seller tenant is behind a self-managed barrier is refused, because the internal service path of SUB-D-125 is not confirmed yet. S-U20 asks the platform for an answer fenced to the target. A write asks the same about every other tenant axis that it sets. So the Orders service identity at the platform root, or a seller principal above its customers, can act in a customer's ordering tenant, and a principal of a sibling customer cannot. A suspended, deleted, unknown or barred tenant is refused as a denial is. Teardown is the exception: `cancel`, `archive` and `release` do not apply the test that the tenant is active. The point decision and the barrier check still apply to them, so an unknown tenant or one behind a barrier stays refused. An offboarded tenant, suspended or deleted, must still be able to end its subscriptions and free its places; otherwise its places keep counting against the payer's occupancy keys. Every other command still refuses an inactive tenant as a denial. A create, a receiver command or a reservation command acts on the line or attempt it names: refused there, it answers 403 and asks and writes nothing. A reserve asks the same about its payer and seller tenants. A lookup answers 404 and reveals nothing, also when the caller has no lookup permission at all. Activation and the lifecycle commands read their subscription by id within that tenant only: a subscription that does not exist there, whether absent, of another tenant or in a tenant the caller may not act in, answers 404 and reveals nothing. The occupancy read asks the same point question in the key's resource tenant (SUB-D-89). An outage or timeout of the decision point or of Tenant Resolver answers 503 `dependency_unavailable` and is never an allow. The Tenant Resolver call runs under the provider call timeout (SUB-D-87) and outside any database transaction. Only a read by id (`GET /subscriptions/{id}`, `GET /operations/{id}`, and the composition reads `GET /subscriptions/{id}/composition` and `/composition/slices`, SUB-D-110) still asks for the listed tenants: it cannot name its tenant before the read, so it asks the decision point for the list of tenants that the grant reaches and is filtered by that list. That list is bounded by the decision point's expansion limit, 10 000 tenants by default; a read by a caller whose grant reaches more tenants is refused (403). The list serves user callers; a service caller reads by id through a point read that names its tenant (SUB-D-125). The gear does not advertise tenant-hierarchy push-down to the decision point.

**SUB-D-84: Receiver command answers.** A `settle` that writes its own receipt stores it `applied`: with reason `settled_no_create` when it records the no-create tombstone, or `already_created` when the attempt's create had applied. A `settle` of an attempt whose line another attempt already holds refuses `capability_closed` (`successor_attempt`, SUB-D-95), and a `settle` of a line bound in another ordering tenant refuses `business_conflict` (`origin_line_bound`, SUB-D-94); both are stored and replay. The settle answer, `AttemptOutcomeV1` as for the exact lookup, carries the attempt key, the stable create result, the subscription reference when one exists, the operation that decided the create result, the requested generation and its state, and `pendingCleanup`. The create result and the operation that decided it are optional members: they are present once a create or settle of the attempt has applied, and absent otherwise. The generation state is absent when the receiver has no record of that generation; that never hides the create result. When `pause` or `revoke` recorded the generation before any create (the tombstone of SUB-D-59), the attempt has a ledger record but no create fence: the exact lookup then answers with no create result and no subscription reference, the requested generation's state (`paused` or `revoked`) and `pendingCleanup` false. The lookup answers 404 only when the receiver has neither a create result for the attempt nor a record of the requested generation. The settle answer also carries the settle's own receipt id when the settle wrote one, as an optional member, so that receipt can be read by its id; a `settle` whose attempt is already final writes none (SUB-D-97). `pause`, `unpause` and `revoke` answer `ReceiverOutcomeV1`: the command's own receipt id, the command, the generation state after it, the attempt's create result and subscription reference once a create or settle of the attempt has applied, and `pendingCleanup`. `pause` of a generation with no record records it `paused`, and `revoke` records it `revoked` (the tombstone of SUB-D-59). `unpause` of a generation with no record is applied and records nothing, so its answer has no generation state. A command on a `revoked` or `settled` generation, `pause` of a `paused` generation and `unpause` of an `open` one are applied and change nothing; the answer reports the current state. `revoke` of the generation of an applied activation records `revoked`, and the subscription stays `active`: stopping it is cancellation. `pendingCleanup` is computed each time an answer is built (first answer, replay, lookup and settle): it is true while the preparation effect of that generation's activation is neither withdrawn nor confirmed. It is never stored; the stored receiver outcome keeps only the command and the generation state after it.

**SUB-D-85: Activation admission.** The activation request names the subscription's ordering tenant (`orderingTenantId`, SUB-D-83) and is authorized in it alone; the subscription is read by id within that tenant. Activation reads the activation profile that the subscription's seller tenant sets for its resource tenant, from the policy set (SUB-D-72): the service profile, the preparation duration and the `before_apply` hook budget, with the profile version. Admission freezes these values on the activation record with the start and the deadline; a later Settings change affects only new admissions. No profile refuses `capability_closed` (`activation_settings`); there is no built-in fallback. A profile out of bounds reads as unreadable settings: a preparation duration outside 1 second to 30 days, a hook budget outside 100 milliseconds to 10 seconds, or a version that is not 1 to 128 visible ASCII characters. Admission then answers `dependency_unavailable`, logs the cause and writes nothing. The `no_preparation` profile runs locally (SUB-D-70). The `entitlements` profile refuses `capability_closed` (`entitlements_preparation`) until its S-U04 preparation provider is configured.

The business identity of an activation is `(subscription, generation)`. Another transport key for that identity returns the existing operation. Another generation while one is `preparing` refuses `business_conflict` (`activation_in_progress`); under the `entitlements` profile an open preparation effect answers first (`effect_in_flight`, SUB-D-88). A new generation after a closed attempt is a successor attempt and refuses `capability_closed` (`successor_activation`) until S-U01 enables succession. The request origin must equal the subscription's origin except for `generation`, and that generation must have a receiver record: `open` admits; `paused` refuses `invalid_state` (`generation_paused`); `revoked` or `settled` refuses `business_conflict` (`generation_closed`); no record refuses `business_conflict` (`generation_unknown`). A draft that holds no accepted terms (SUB-D-109) then refuses `invalid_state` (`acceptance_missing`) after the revision check: it is stored, and nothing is asked for it, not even its activation profile. After these checks, the line's reservation, or what the line's other origin versions do, decides the path to a place (SUB-D-102).

`requestedStart` is a lower bound. When it is absent, or at most 5 seconds after the server's current time, preparation starts at once. A later start refuses `capability_closed` (`future_start`): a protected future start needs S-U10 and the AP03 commitment.

**SUB-D-86: Activation evidence and resolution.** After admission, outside any transaction, a drive collects and stages the evidence: the tenant states, the fresh execution check (SUB-D-60), the financial coverage (S-U07) and, under the `entitlements` profile, readiness. Tenant Resolver must report the payer, resource and seller tenants active; a suspended, deleted or unknown tenant closes the attempt with `tenant_inactive`, and an unreachable resolver makes it wait. Covered financial coverage is consumed and never assessed again; refused coverage closes the attempt with `financial_refused`; pending or unknown coverage waits. An execution refusal closes the attempt with its own reason; an authorization denial by the check closes it with `policy_denied`. A definitive preparation failure closes it with `preparation_failed`. After the `before_apply` stage allows and before the decision, the drive asks Tenant Resolver again about the ordering tenant: a tenant that is no longer active or no longer known closes the attempt with `tenant_inactive`, and a recorded grant anchor that no longer reaches it closes the attempt with `policy_denied` (SUB-D-114).

A closure records one reason: `deadline_expired`, `preparation_failed`, `withdrawn`, `hook_vetoed`, `tenant_inactive`, `occupancy_full`, `financial_refused`, `policy_denied`, `key_movement_unsupported` (at the decision the claim set names other keys than the line's reservation holds, SUB-D-102), `subscription_cancelled` (a cancel of the preparing subscription, which leaves it `cancelled`, SUB-D-105), or an execution refusal: `hold_expired`, `price_closed`, `sku_retired`, `market_changed`, `acceptance_mismatch` or `activation_outside_accepted_window`. The Orders mapping is set only where this design fixes it: `hold_expired` maps to `order-binding-expired` and `market_changed` to `market-divergence`; other reasons carry no mapping until S-U01 agrees one. A wait records a blocking cause: `dependency_unavailable`, `evidence_missing`, `provider_corrupt`, `unmapped_result`, `occupancy_full`, `financial_pending` or `readiness_pending`. A deadline closure keeps the last blocking cause as its `cause`. A pause is never a cause, because the deadline clock is stopped while the generation is paused.

The operation read shows the activation progress: phase, service profile, frozen profile version, deadline, whether the generation is paused, readiness, reason, Orders mapping, cause and T. The subscription read and the `after_applied` outcome carry `serviceActivatedAt`. A veto keeps `hook_vetoed` as the closure reason; the hook instance and its reason go to the refusal detail of the audit record.

**SUB-D-87: Activation driver.** A polling driver resumes every unresolved activation: after a restart, a retryable wait, an unpause, or a request that ran out of its budget. It reads the activation records that are due by their next check time and takes a lease on each one. It does not run on the outbox, because one waiting activation would hold back its whole outbox partition. Correctness never depends on the lease: every resolution is a conditional write on the record's decision version, so two drives of one activation commit at most one result.

The driver reads readiness from Entitlements by operation and posture revision; an inbound `ReadinessObservationV1` only wakes the driver for its operation. A drive calls every provider as the gear's own service principal, under the ordering tenant's scope that the decision point issues for that principal (SUB-D-123); the caller's context only authorizes the admission. A provider that refuses the gear's own identity is treated as unavailable, not as a denial of the purchase.

One drive has a budget of 20 seconds. It runs at most three owner stages one after another (dispatch, readiness, then the evidence set, whose calls run at the same time) and then the `before_apply` stage. Each owner call is cut at the provider call timeout. The configuration must keep three provider call timeouts plus the largest hook budget (10 seconds) at least 1 second below the drive budget, so the decision transaction always has time; a longer timeout is refused at startup.

A drive that waits writes its next check time: a random wait from zero up to 2^n seconds (full jitter), capped by the maximum back-off and never after the deadline. A drive that fails, runs out of its budget or panics writes its next check time over its lease, in its own conditional write on the record as it stands, and keeps the decision version. It records the cause `dependency_unavailable` only over no cause or `dependency_unavailable`, so a stored business cause such as `occupancy_full` survives to the deadline closure. Once the deadline has passed, the wait is the plain back-off.

The driver runs two workers, one for drives and one for withdrawals, each with up to `concurrency` items at a time. A failing or panicking item does not end its pass, and the driver counts drives that run out of their budget or panic. Each discovery read, of the due activations and of the due withdrawals, runs within the budget of one drive. A read still running then is dropped and fails its pass with that cause. The database is sent no cancel, so the statement runs on and holds its connection until it ends; a PostgreSQL deployment bounds that with the database's `statement_timeout`. A pass fails only when its discovery fails; a failed pass is logged with its causes and counted `discovery_failed`. A panicking pass is logged and counted `panicked`. After either, the worker waits a jittered back-off that starts at 1 second and doubles up to the maximum back-off; the doubling cannot overflow, so a worker never stops because of its failures. The posture worker and the period reconciler bound their discovery reads in the same way (SUB-D-106, SUB-D-119). A worker that ends before the gear stops is logged as an error. On stop, a pass in flight starts no new item and gets half of the gear's stop timeout; the gear then waits for the lease releases of dropped requests and stops the outbox last. A request that drives its own activation holds the lease for its request budget; if the request is dropped, the lease is given back. The jitter source is injected, so tests can fix it.

**SUB-D-88: Effect records and the in-flight marker.** Every remote effect of an operation has a durable effect record. The first kind is the preparation of an activation under the `entitlements` profile. An effect moves `dispatch_pending → dispatched → confirmed`, or to `withdraw_requested → withdrawn`; it becomes `compensation_required` when a prepared effect outlives an activation that cannot commit. A non-terminal effect is the subscription's in-flight marker: another activation of the same subscription refuses `business_conflict` (`effect_in_flight`), which is retryable (SUB-D-82). The closure commit marks the effect `withdraw_requested`; the withdrawal worker sends the withdrawal only after that commit and asks again until the owner takes it. A late ready for a closed activation is recorded on its effect and changes nothing else; if the withdrawal was already taken, the effect becomes `compensation_required` and the withdrawal is sent again. A work-order timeout leaves the effect unresolved, never `withdrawn`. The marker gates only another activation of the subscription. An archive does not wait for it: the withdrawal and the compensation of a closed activation's effect go on whatever the subscription's status (SUB-D-72). Posture rows are not effect records and never enter the marker (SUB-D-106).

**SUB-D-89: Occupancy at activation.** The occupancy policy supplies the claim set of a subscription: its keys, in the stored form of SUB-D-63, each with an explicit `maxConcurrentActive` and source, and the policy version (SUB-D-63). No policy refuses `capability_closed` (`occupancy_policy`); there is no default limit. Each key has one counter record with its active, retained-suspended and order-reserved counts. Activation admission checks capacity without claiming: a full key refuses `occupancy_full`, which is retryable (SUB-D-82). The activation decision locks the key records in key order and claims every key atomically with the activation commit. A key that is full at the decision is not a refusal: the activation waits with the cause `occupancy_full` until a place frees, or the deadline closes it with the reason `occupancy_full`. This is the unreserved path; an activation of a line that holds a reservation takes no capacity preflight and converts the reservation's own places at the decision (SUB-D-102). `GET /subscriptions/v1/occupancy` reads one key: its active, retained and reserved counts, its limit and the policy version. It needs `subscription_read` in the resource tenant, and a key with no record answers 404.

**SUB-D-90: Lock order.** A transaction takes the records it needs in one order: the order line's lock, the receiver ledger record, the create fence, the subscription, the activation record, the suspension causes, the line's reservation, then occupancy keys sorted by their stored key (SUB-D-63), then the commercial rows: the fact versions, the ordering tenant's journal head and the period cursor (SUB-D-118). Each command takes a subset of these in that order. The line lock is one row per order line of an ordering tenant, across its origin versions, inserted if absent and then locked. A reserve, a release and an activation admission of the line take it first of all, so they serialize even before the line has a reservation, a draft or an activation (SUB-D-100 to SUB-D-102). The activation decision takes no line lock: it starts at the receiver ledger record and locks the line's reservation after the activation. A release takes no subscription lock. Create and settle lock the generation's existing receiver ledger record before they write the create fence; a create locks it before it reads the fence too. A create under a `revoked` or `settled` generation refuses `business_conflict` (`generation_closed`), and one under a `paused` or `open` generation proceeds and changes nothing on the record. Admission, settle, pause, unpause, revoke and the activation decision lock the receiver ledger record before the subscription, and every ledger write is conditional on the state read under that lock. Composition rows take no lock of their own: a create inserts them with its draft, and the activation decision under the subscription's lock (SUB-D-109).

**SUB-D-91: Stored record formats.** A stored record with structure (revision snapshot, receipt outcome, stored refusal, staged evidence, receiver outcome, accepted evidence document) carries a format version. A record gains a new optional member without a new format version: a reader treats a missing optional member as absent, and an unknown format version fails to read. The persistence layer owns these formats and their encoding; the domain layer uses typed values and holds no storage encoding. Payload, posture, allocation and evidence digests are domain logic: SHA-256 over canonical JSON built from the normalized domain values, so a change of storage encoding cannot change a digest.

**SUB-D-92: Steps of other owners are owner requirements.** A feature step that describes only what another owner does cannot be built by Subscriptions. As for the valuation rules (SUB-D-75), such text is an owner requirement in UPSTREAM_REQS, not a feature step. `cpt-cf-bss-subscriptions-upreq-acquisition-owner-steps` holds the capture-start decision of Orders Lifecycle, the capture identity of Orders Lifecycle and Payments, capture against the reservation by Billing and Payments, capture deduplication by Payments, the outcomes of cancellation against capture start, the release of every held place by Orders Lifecycle, and the Workflow spawn signal of partial orders. Feature 01 keeps the Subscriptions side of each: its own fences, reservations and recovery. A requirement becomes binding when its owner accepts it in its own register; the affected profile stays gated until then.

**SUB-D-93: Immediate write transactions on SQLite.** A Subscriptions command reads its receipt, fence and ledger records and then writes in the same transaction, while the outbox workers write on other connections. On SQLite, a deferred transaction that started as a reader cannot become a writer after another connection has written: it fails at once with `SQLITE_BUSY_SNAPSHOT` instead of waiting under `busy_timeout`. A deployment that runs this gear on SQLite must set `transaction_mode: immediate` on that database server, so a command takes the write lock when it begins and waits for it. A read runs in one read-only transaction and takes no write lock. On PostgreSQL that transaction is `REPEATABLE READ`, so every query of one read sees the same snapshot: an operation read takes its receipt and its activation progress from the same commits. A command runs in `READ COMMITTED` there. PostgreSQL needs no further setting.

**SUB-D-94: One origin line binds one subscription in the platform.** The create fence and its no-create tombstone are unique on the business key `(originType, originId, originVersion, attemptId, lineId)` without the ordering tenant, and the receiver ledger is unique on that key plus `generation`, also without the tenant. So one origin line binds at most one subscription in the whole platform, whichever ordering tenant a request names. The ordering tenant stays on every row, and every read and write of a command still runs in the caller's tenant scope. A create, `settle`, `pause`, `unpause` or `revoke` whose origin line holds a fence, or whose generation holds a ledger record, in another ordering tenant refuses `business_conflict` with the reason `origin_line_bound`. The refusal is terminal: it is stored as the failed receipt with its audit record and replays under its key. It reveals only that the line is bound, with no id and no tenant. A create that the caller's tenant already applied is recovered before this check (SUB-D-97). For this reason the permissions that can bind a line are restricted (SUB-D-96). The order-line reservation is not part of this rule: it is kept per ordering tenant (SUB-D-100). The fence needs uniqueness across tenants, not only within one ordering tenant: the caller names the ordering tenant, so the same line could otherwise be bound in a second tenant. The global unique indexes on the create fence and the receiver ledger enforce it; the gear never reads across tenants for the fence. A create or a receiver command inserts its fence or ledger row and does nothing on a conflict. A conflict whose winner its own single-target scope does not show means that the line is bound in another tenant, and the command refuses `origin_line_bound`.

**SUB-D-95: One fence per origin line.** A line holds one create fence, whatever its attempt: the first create or `settle` of any attempt of the line writes it, as a created result or as the no-create tombstone. A create or `settle` of another attempt of a line that already holds a fence, created or settled, is a successor attempt. It refuses `capability_closed` (`successor_attempt`), stored and replayed under its key, and the settle writes no second tombstone. Until the successor protocol opens under S-U01, the Orders Workflow therefore cannot settle a second abandoned attempt of one line. Succession can change this only through the fenced successor protocol of SUB-D-33.

**SUB-D-96: Permissions of the receiver path.** The receiver commands use the operation resource type `gts.cf.bss.subscriptions.operation.v1~` with the actions `settle_attempt`, `pause_attempt`, `unpause_attempt` and `revoke_attempt`. The exact attempt lookup uses its `recover` action, and the operation read `GET /operations/{operationId}` uses its `read` action (`operation_read`). The order-line reservations use the same resource type with the actions `reserve_place` and `release_place` (`operation_reserve_place`, `operation_release_place`), and their exact lookup uses `recover` (`operation_recover`). The gear does not check which service calls create, `settle`, `pause`, `unpause`, `revoke`, `reserve` or `release` until a deployment configures the Orders service principal for the caller check of SUB-D-127 (S-U01). Until then, "Orders only" is policy configuration: `subscription_create`, the four receiver permissions and the two reservation permissions are granted only to the Orders service account, and never in a default or customer role. The reason is the platform-wide fence of SUB-D-94: a principal that holds one of the create or receiver permissions in any tenant could bind or settle an origin line there, and so block that order line for every tenant. A reservation, its tombstone and its line lock are records of one ordering tenant, so a holder of the reservation permissions cannot block another tenant's line; but a reserve holds places against that tenant's occupancy limits. The reservation permissions stay policy-only until the S-U01 caller check, as `subscription_create` does, with one recorded risk: a holder of `operation_release_place` can tombstone a line of a tenant that its grant reaches before the order reserves it, and that tombstone refuses the line's later reserve and activation (`reservation_released`, SUB-D-101, SUB-D-102). The grant therefore goes to the Orders service account only; an operator holds the same release only for the incident path of SUB-D-101.

**SUB-D-97: A recovery writes nothing.** A recovery returns the retained outcome and writes nothing: no receipt under its key, no audit record and no revision. There are exactly three recoveries. The first is a create recovered under a new transport key, answered before Pricing is asked (SUB-D-108). A create that the caller's tenant already applied is recovered before the platform-wide binding check of SUB-D-94: it returns the retained outcome and writes nothing, even when another tenant has recorded a later generation of that line. This is true under any generation: a create recovered under a later generation opens no receiver ledger record for that generation. The ledger holds a record only for the generation under which the create applied and for each generation that a receiver command named, so an activation under such a later generation refuses as SUB-D-85 says for a generation with no record. The second is a `settle` whose attempt is already final: the attempt's create result is decided and its generation is already `revoked` or `settled`. Its answer reports the current state and names no settle receipt (SUB-D-84). The third is a reserve under a new key that finds the same subject's reservation of the line, held or consumed: it answers that reservation with its original reserve receipt (SUB-D-100). Because nothing is stored under the request's key, a later request under that key is judged afresh. A recovery is not an operation, so it writes no audit record (SUB-D-76). `pause`, `unpause` and `revoke` are not recoveries: each writes its own `applied` receipt and audit record even when it changes nothing, so its key is bound and a later different body under that key conflicts (SUB-D-84). Neither is a release of a consumed or released line: it writes its own `applied` receipt (SUB-D-101).

**SUB-D-98: Delivery of `after_applied`.** The outbox delivers an applied outcome to the `after_applied` hooks at least once under the key `(operationId, revision)`, up to an attempt cap per message, the bounded setting `hooks.max_attempts` (DESIGN §3.8). The cap counts the deliveries of one message, not of a batch. A delivery that the outbox lease cuts short counts as a try of its message. A message behind it in the same lease, which no delivery started, is not charged for that lease and is never dead-lettered for it. The hooks of one outcome are called at the same time, each under its own call deadline (`hooks.call_timeout_ms`), so a hung hook fails alone and one delivery lasts at most one deadline. If a hook fails or times out, the whole delivery is tried again, so every hook must be idempotent on the key. A message that reaches the cap is dead-lettered in the outbox's dead-letter store, durably, naming the hooks that failed, and no longer holds back its partition. An operator replays it through the outbox's dead-letter replay.

**SUB-D-99: A command that loses every race.** A command runs in its own transaction. When a concurrent command commits the same command key, create fence or ledger record first, the run rolls back and the next run reads what the winner committed. The same holds when a conditional write finds a locked row moved, or when a reserve meets the place that another origin version of its line committed first: the next run answers what it then reads (SUB-D-100). The lifecycle and reservation commands run this way too. A command runs at most three times. When every run loses, it answers the retryable `concurrent_modification` (409, category `Aborted`) and stores nothing: no receipt, no audit record and no revision. The caller retries with the same key and body, and the retry is judged afresh. On SQLite a busy error on a command's write counts as a lost race and is retried in the same way (SUB-D-93).

**SUB-D-100: Order-line reservation.** At order confirmation and before capture, Orders Lifecycle reserves the place of an order line with the SDK method `reserve` (SUB-D-127). The reservation is keyed by the line `(originType, originId, originVersion, lineId)` in the ordering tenant that the request names, without attempt or generation (SUB-D-71). It is unique per ordering tenant, and in one tenant at most one origin version of a line holds or consumed a place. It is not platform-wide like the create fence of SUB-D-94. Orders Lifecycle reserves and releases a line in the one ordering tenant that orders it, the tenant of the line's create; a reserve of the line in another tenant is outside this contract, and the gear does not detect it. A reserve reads the line's reservations and created fences in its own tenant only. In another tenant it therefore finds no subscription of the line, of any origin version, and no reservation that the ordering tenant holds: it is judged as if the line had no subscription, so none of the refusals that a subscription of the line causes (`activation_in_progress`, `line_active`, `version_amendment`) applies. Its places stay in its own tenant, no create of the line can apply there (`origin_line_bound`) and so no activation consumes them, and only a release in that tenant frees them. A reservation holds one reserved place on each key of the line's claim set (SUB-D-63). The key counts it as `reserved` until the line's activation consumes it (SUB-D-102) or a fenced release frees it (SUB-D-101). Its states are `held`, `consumed` and `released`; a release tombstone is a `released` reservation with no place. A reservation is the line's own: it creates no draft, no service fact and no outbox entry, and the draft that the line's activation attaches to it later allocates nothing.

The request names the line's subject: its tenant axes and its typed composition (SUB-D-107), as a create of the line sends them. The subject digest covers the composition in its canonical form, and a reserve asks Pricing nothing. A reservation held before compositions were typed keeps the subject digest of its raw composition object, so a reserve or an activation of its line with a typed composition refuses `reservation_conflict` unless that object was already in the canonical form; it is released and its purchase ordered again (SUB-D-109). The ordering tenant must be the resource tenant (DESIGN §2.2). Outside any transaction the reserve asks the occupancy policy for the claim set, then the purchase commitment owner (S-U01) whether a durable commitment covers the line. A missing policy refuses `capability_closed` (`occupancy_policy`), and a missing commitment owner `capability_closed` (`purchase_commitment`). The transaction then takes the line lock (SUB-D-90) and the line's reservation, and decides in this order. A line of an origin other than Orders refuses `origin_disabled`. A released or tombstoned line refuses `business_conflict` (`reservation_released`): a delayed reserve takes no place. A held or consumed reservation of the same subject is recovered with its original receipt (SUB-D-97), and one of another subject refuses `business_conflict` (`reservation_conflict`). Another origin version of the line that holds or consumed a place refuses `capability_closed` (`version_amendment`) until the S-U01/S-U10 version mapping exists. The line counts one place across its versions. A subscription of the line, of any version, that prepares without a reservation refuses `business_conflict` (`activation_in_progress`). One of this version that went live without a reservation refuses `business_conflict` (`line_active`), and one of another version `capability_closed` (`version_amendment`). Both refusals of another version hold after that version's subscription ended: a subscription cancelled or archived after it went live keeps its activation `applied`, and its reservation, if it consumed one, stays `consumed`, so another version of the line is refused `version_amendment` until the S-U01/S-U10 version mapping exists. No durable commitment refuses `invalid_state` (`commitment_missing`). A key of the claim set at the current policy's limit refuses `occupancy_full`. Of these, `origin_disabled`, `reservation_released`, `reservation_conflict`, `line_active` and `commitment_missing` are final and stored; the others store nothing (SUB-D-82). Otherwise the reserve commits the held reservation and its places with the receipt and the audit record. The reservation keeps the commitment reference and version, the subject digest and the claim-set version its places were admitted under. The exact lookup, an SDK method (SUB-D-127), returns the reservation of a line, a tombstone included, with every place it holds or held.

**SUB-D-101: Fenced release of an order-line place.** Orders Lifecycle releases a line's place with the SDK method `release` (SUB-D-127), naming the ordering tenant and the line. Only this release frees a held place: a settle, a draft void, a closed activation, a deadline or the passing of time leaves it held. Orders Lifecycle always releases a held place that no activation of the line will consume. There is no timeout and no sweeper: a place that Orders does not release is freed in an incident by the same `release`, granted to an operator, and the capture fence still guards it. Outside any transaction, and only for a held place, the release asks the capture fence owner (S-U01/S-U07) by the reservation's original identities. The owner answers resolved, with no capture or with the capture and its remedy, once it acknowledges that no further capture of the line runs; otherwise it answers unresolved. A missing capture fence owner refuses a held place `capability_closed` (`capture_fence`). The transaction refuses a line of an origin other than Orders `origin_disabled`, stored, with no tombstone. It then takes the line lock and the line's reservation, and decides by what it finds. With no reservation, it records the line's tombstone, a `released` reservation with no place, and asks no owner, so a release works before any reserve or attempt exists (AP-C13). A held reservation whose line has a preparing activation refuses `business_conflict` (`activation_in_progress`): Orders revokes or settles that generation first. A held reservation whose capture is not resolved, or that the fence was not asked about, refuses `business_conflict` (`capture_unresolved`). Neither refusal stores anything (SUB-D-82). A resolved capture releases the reservation: its places are freed, and the capture and remedy references and the owner's record version stay on it. A consumed reservation is answered unchanged with reason `already_consumed` and the subscription that consumed it; that subscription ends through its own cancel. A released reservation, a tombstone included, is answered unchanged with reason `already_released`. Each of these applied answers writes its own receipt and audit record. A release writes no outbox entry.

**SUB-D-102: Activation of an order line.** An activation admission takes its line lock first of all (SUB-D-90). After the subscription and its activations, it reads the line's reservation in the subscription's ordering tenant. A held reservation of another subject, whose subject digest differs from the subscription's tenant axes and composition digested as a reserve digests them (SUB-D-100), refuses `business_conflict` (`reservation_conflict`), stored, as a reserve's own `reservation_conflict` is. A held reservation of the subscription's subject on exactly the keys of the admission's claim set is the reserved path: admission takes no capacity preflight and records the reservation on the activation. The decision locks the reservation after the activation, converts it to `consumed` and each of its places from reserved to active without a capacity check, and then samples T. A held reservation on other keys refuses `capability_closed` (`key_movement`) at admission, because no key moves in this version (S-U10). A claim set that names other keys at the decision closes the activation with `key_movement_unsupported`; it does not wait. A released or tombstoned reservation refuses `business_conflict` (`reservation_released`), and a consumed one `business_conflict` (`reservation_consumed`); both are stored. Every closure leaves the reservation held. A line without a reservation is judged across its origin versions, as a reserve judges it. Another version that holds or consumed a reservation, or whose subscription went live without one, refuses `capability_closed` (`version_amendment`), also after that version's subscription was cancelled or archived, until that mapping exists (SUB-D-100). Another version whose subscription prepares refuses `business_conflict` (`activation_in_progress`). Neither stores anything (SUB-D-82). Otherwise the line takes the unreserved path of SUB-D-89.

**SUB-D-103: Lifecycle commands.** `suspend`, `resume`, `cancel` and `archive` act on an existing subscription: `POST /subscriptions/v1/subscriptions/{id}/suspend`, `/resume`, `/cancel` and `/archive`. The body names the ordering tenant (SUB-D-83), `If-Match` carries the expected revision (SUB-D-80), and an `Idempotency-Key` is required. Each command runs in the same order. Its request is checked first, a cancel's mode and reason included (SUB-D-105). Then it is authorized in the named tenant and reads its subscription by id there only. A stored receipt of its key answers again. Outside any transaction, a capability that the command needs and no owner runs refuses `capability_closed` before any effect, whatever the status; an archive asks its lifecycle settings at this point too (SUB-D-72). A read whose status has no edge for the command, or whose revision differs, asks no owner and runs no hook. Otherwise the owners are asked one after another, each within the owner call timeout, and an owner's answer may refuse the command. Then the `before_apply` hooks run within `lifecycle.hook_budget` on an immutable snapshot (SUB-D-73): the operation id named before the stage, the command, the subscription at its expected revision, the digest of the owners' answers and the cancel reason, the profile version `lifecycle.v1` (an archive's policy version instead), and the hook set version. Last, one transaction locks the rows (SUB-D-90) and decides again on what it locked. It commits the new status at the next revision with the claims, the suspension causes, the posture row (SUB-D-106), the receipt, the audit record and the applied outcome on the outbox together.

A wrong source status refuses `invalid_state`, with the status as its detail; a different revision refuses `revision_conflict`; a veto refuses `hook_vetoed` with the hook instance and its reason in the audit record. These refusals, and an owner's refusal such as `archive_dwell`, are decided on the locked rows and stored. A closed capability, an owner that cannot be read, a hook that fails or does not answer within its budget, and a snapshot that no longer matches store nothing (SUB-D-82). The snapshot no longer matches when the locked row would apply although no owner was asked or no hook ran, or when its status, its revision or the hook set changed since the hooks ran. The command then answers `dependency_unavailable` (`before_apply_hooks`), and a retry takes a fresh snapshot. An Orders cancel with `order_compensation` runs the hooks as any caller does: only the gear's own triggers skip them (SUB-D-73). A command is always resolved in its request: 200 with the subscription, the receipt and the new `ETag`, never 202. The receipt names the command, the status after it, the revision, a cancel's reason and an archive's policy version; it names no acquisition origin. One command makes at most three calls one after another, each within the owner call timeout: the tenant check (SUB-D-83) and two owner stages. The configuration keeps those three calls plus `lifecycle.hook_budget` at least 1 second below the 20-second drive budget (SUB-D-87), or the gear refuses to start. The policy decision and the transaction runs are outside that sum.

**SUB-D-104: Suspend and resume.** Suspend moves `active → suspended`. In one commit its active places become retained ones, one suspension cause of kind `requested` opens, and the `restrict` posture row is recorded. Resume moves `suspended → active`. Outside its transaction it asks the occupancy policy for the subscription's claim set. No policy refuses `capability_closed` (`occupancy_policy`). A claim set that names other keys than the places the subscription retains refuses `capability_closed` (`key_movement`): no key moves in this version (S-U10), and the place stays retained. Neither refusal writes anything. In one commit the retained places become active again without a capacity check, each key records the current policy's limit and version, every blocking suspension cause is discharged, and the `reinstate` posture row is recorded. A conversion locks its keys in sorted order and never competes for another place (SUB-D-63). In this version every cause is `requested`. Feature 04 adds the financial causes and their resume refusal, and features 04 and 06 add the payment, term and pending-conversion guards. Both commands need the posture owner (SUB-D-106).

**SUB-D-105: Cancel by source status.** A cancel names one reason by its GTS instance id and at most one mode (SUB-D-01, SUB-D-73). A scheduled mode or a vendor reason refuses `capability_closed` before anything else. A reason that a caller may not send, or text that names no cancel reason, is an invalid request on `reason`. The commit then depends on the status it locked. A `draft` is voided: it becomes `cancelled` with no place, no posture and no owner asked, and the reservation of its order line stays held. The void needs no owner, so it is open in every deployment. A `preparing` subscription is cancelled in one transaction that locks the receiver ledger record, the subscription and the activation in that order, as the activation decision does. The activation closes with the reason `subscription_cancelled`, its preparation effects are to be withdrawn (SUB-D-88), and its operation fails as SUB-D-81 states. One revision moves `preparing → cancelled`, and that revision names the cancel's operation. The reservation stays held. An `active` or `suspended` subscription needs the posture owner and the retention disposition, which must support the offboarding now. Otherwise the cancel refuses `capability_closed` (`lifecycle_posture` or `offboarding`) and writes nothing. Its own places are released in the commit, its blocking causes voided and the `revoke` posture row recorded. In every case the subscription keeps the commit instant as `cancelledAt` and the reason; the receipt, the audit record and the applied outcome keep the reason too. A refused, vetoed or failed cancel moves no place. A cancelled subscription is archived under SUB-D-72.

**SUB-D-106: Lifecycle posture rows and their worker.** An applied suspend, resume or cancel of an `active` or `suspended` subscription records one posture row in its commit: `restrict`, `reinstate` or `revoke`, for the subscription at its new revision. The row is the durable publication intent of SUB-D-30, unique per subscription and revision. In the same commit it supersedes every open row of the subscription at a lower revision. A posture worker sends the row to the posture owner after the commit and observes the owner's completion separately: a row moves `pending → dispatched → observed`, or becomes `superseded`. Only the owner's report that the posture is applied makes a row `observed`. A failure that the owner reports keeps the row `dispatched` with the outcome `failed` and a bounded note, and the worker observes it again; a failure never counts as applied. Once the owner has taken a row, the gear never sends its revision to apply again, so the gear never applies a revision twice: the posture owner retries its own failed apply, and the overdue warning below is the escalation of a failure that does not clear. The worker runs beside the activation driver's workers, as the gear's own service principal (SUB-D-123), and uses the same `activation_driver` settings (SUB-D-87). Its discovery of the due rows runs within the budget of one drive: a discovery that fails or runs past it fails the pass, which is logged with its causes, counted `unavailable` and backed off as a failed pass of the driver is. Every error, timeout and panic of a row writes its next attempt with a jittered back-off, so a failing row never stays at the head of the queue. A row that cannot be read backs off unread; a row the database is only too busy to read stays due. Posture rows hold no lease, so a row read before a newer row superseded it can still be sent once. The owner contract therefore orders postures by revision: the owner applies a posture only when its revision is newer than the last one it applied for the subscription, it is idempotent per subscription and revision, and it retries its own failed apply. A row that backs off more than `activation_driver.posture_overdue_after` times in one state is reported overdue: one warning from the back-off whose committed write crosses the limit, then an `overdue` count on every later back-off. The row is still sent or observed again, and its attempts count anew in each state. Posture rows never enter the in-flight marker of SUB-D-88. Until feature 05 provides a posture owner, none is configured: suspend, resume and a cancel of an `active` or `suspended` subscription refuse `capability_closed` (`lifecycle_posture`) before any effect, so no posture row is written. The worker still runs.

**SUB-D-107: Typed composition of an order line.** A create and a reserve name the composition of their order line as `CompositionV1`: the Pricing acceptance `acceptance` (`acceptanceId` and `termsDigest`), the plan `planId`, its published revision `planRevisionId`, the `quantity` and the selected cells `selections`, each an `itemId` with an optional `dimensionValue`. Every member is required except `dimensionValue`. A member that the composition does not define, a brand or PlanTier member included, is refused as an unknown member with the status that the request decoder gives: on REST that is 422, as for every unknown member of a request body of this gear. The SDK decodes the acceptance reference with a type that the answers share, so there an unknown member of `acceptance` reads as absent. The bounds are: a terms digest of 64 lowercase hex characters; a quantity written as digits with an optional fraction (`^[0-9]+(\.[0-9]+)?$`), at most 40 characters, positive and at most 28 significant digits, whose trailing fraction zeros are read as absent; 1 to 256 selections, each item at most once; and a dimension value of 1 to 255 characters without a control character. A bound that fails is an invalid request on `composition` whose detail names the member's path, for example `selections[2].dimension_value` on REST, and nothing is written. The gear keeps the composition in one canonical form: the quantity as normalized decimal text, the selections sorted by item id and then by dimension value in UTF-16 code units, as Pricing sorts cells, and an absent dimension value left out. So `"2.50"` and `"2.5"` are one purchase. The stored composition, the create's payload digest and the reserve's subject digest use that form (SUB-D-100). `bindingRef` and `externalOrderRef` stay the caller's provenance; they are not evidence.

**SUB-D-108: Accepted terms bound at create.** A create freezes the terms that Pricing accepted for its order line. It reads the receipt of the acceptance it names with `PricingAcceptanceV1::acceptance`, in the catalog of its seller tenant. It reads only the issued receipt, never a fresh resolution of terms. It reads as the gear's one service principal (SUB-D-126), never as the caller, through Pricing's service read path: a pinned read in the catalog of the seller tenant that the create names, under that tenant's single-target scope, and the receipt must confirm that seller. The caller's reach over the seller tenant is checked first, with the create's other tenant axes (SUB-D-83). The gear obtains the principal's context at each read (UPSTREAM_REQS §2.2). A missing principal, or a Pricing without its service read path, is a closed capability: until both exist, every create refuses `capability_closed` (`accepted_terms`, 501) and stores nothing. The read runs once, outside any transaction, within the owner call timeout (SUB-D-87). The Pricing client is resolved at each call, so the gear can start before Pricing or run without it.

A create runs in this order. It checks its key and its request (SUB-D-107) and authorizes the caller in the ordering tenant (SUB-D-83). In one read-only transaction, a replay of the key and a create that the business key holds with the same payload answer from storage (SUB-D-97). Only then is Pricing asked, and the create's transaction decides last. A create of a disabled origin asks Pricing nothing. So while Pricing, its service read path or the gear's principal is closed or unreachable, a create answers the closed capability or the retryable unavailability before any stored refusal that its transaction would give, such as `payload_changed` or `origin_line_bound`. A replay and a recovery still answer.

Pricing's answer maps by category. No Pricing client refuses `capability_closed` (`accepted_terms`, 501), and no principal is then asked for. No principal provisioned for the gear refuses the same. Not found is an acceptance that Pricing does not hold. A principal that is misprovisioned (not a service subject homed in the platform-root tenant) or that cannot be obtained (its exchange fails), and Pricing's permission denied or unauthenticated, refuse the gear's own principal, never the purchase: `dependency_unavailable` (503). The gear logs each of these at warn once per seller, with the seller tenant, for an operator to fix. Any other error and a timeout answer `dependency_unavailable` (503). None of these stores anything.

A receipt is first checked against itself with Pricing's public digest functions. Its acceptance id is the one asked, and its seller is the catalog asked. Every instant has an RFC 3339 form in UTC. Its BillingTerms are of schema 1 and match their digest. Every binding has an approved price whose money and template digests are its own; a usage binding has its rating policy and its meter, a recurring binding its period, and a policy digest matches its content. The terms and request digests are Pricing's own over the query and the bindings. Then the typed terms are bounded: each item selected once, exactly one binding per selected cell, each in the market's currency; a currency of 1 to 16 visible ASCII characters; a region that is not blank, kept as given; order, SKU and policy versions of 1 or more. The values used as identifiers refuse control characters: the order and line ids (1 to 128 characters), the SKU code (1 to 255) and the dimension values. Evidence strings and keys must not hold U+0000, the one character that a JSON column cannot store; any other owner text is frozen as given. The evidence document is at most 8 MiB of canonical JSON. A receipt that breaks one of these rules is a corrupt owner answer: `dependency_unavailable`, nothing stored and the key not used up. It is never a refusal of the purchase.

The byte bound comes from the published caps of Pricing and Products (Pricing D-457, Products P-D-225), with headroom. A plan revision has at most 200 items, so a receipt has at most 200 bindings. A code is capped at 64 characters, a SKU name at 200, a GL code, a tax category and a metering unit at 64, a meter reference and its version at 512, and an invoice line template at 2,000. Canonical JSON writes a character in at most six bytes (an escaped control character), or four in an identifier. With every text and every number at its widest, a receipt with one-band price ladders and no region is 4,481,245 bytes. Pricing caps neither the bands of a tier ladder nor the market region. The rest of the bound admits ladders of 236 bands on all 200 bindings with a region of 255 characters. A larger receipt is corrupt. A cap on both is an open point for Pricing, as are the texts stored before Pricing's caps, which those caps never judge (UPSTREAM_REQS §2.2).

The create's transaction applies its other rules first (SUB-D-33, SUB-D-59, SUB-D-71, SUB-D-94, SUB-D-95). Then an acceptance that Pricing does not hold refuses `invalid_state` (`acceptance_missing`). A receipt that accepted something else refuses `business_conflict` (`acceptance_mismatch`). The first part that differs is the audit refusal detail, checked in this order: `order_line` (order id, version and line, as text), `tenants` (payer and resource tenant), `plan` (plan and revision), `quantity` (by value), `selections` (as sets) and `terms_digest` (against `acceptance.termsDigest`). The seller is not compared there: a receipt of another seller than the catalog asked is a corrupt answer, as above. Both refusals are final: they are stored with their audit record and replay under their key. A create does not check the hold expiry: eligibility at activation is the fresh execution check of SUB-D-60. Otherwise the draft's commit freezes the terms with the draft (SUB-D-109).

**SUB-D-109: Accepted composition history.** A create's commit writes the accepted target beside the draft: one accepted terms record per acceptance of the subscription, one component and its first lot. The terms record keeps the acceptance reference, the catalog, the request, terms and evidence digests, the accepted instant, the currency, the region and the evidence document. The document is Pricing's complete receipt, frozen as a versioned record (`v` 1, snake_case members, normalized decimals, lowercase hex digests, RFC 3339 instants in UTC). Each instant in it, the accepted instant included, is frozen as Pricing gave it; only the typed column of the accepted instant is floored to the millisecond (SUB-D-111). `evidence_digest` is SHA-256 over its canonical JSON, taken in the domain (SUB-D-91). A read of a document checks it against its digest and its typed columns; a mismatch is a corrupt row. Products is not called: the dated SKU descriptors arrive in the bindings as accepted.

Current Pricing has no optional items, so an acquisition line is one component of kind `plan` with the line key `plan#1`, and its stable item ids are the plan's items as bound. A component keeps its id across quantity changes and is never a charge-occurrence id (SUB-D-28). A lot is unique per purchase business identity: the subscription, the purchase kind `acquisition` and the origin line (origin type, id, version and line, without attempt or generation). A create recovered under another key adds no lot. A reserve, a create, a draft cancel and a closed activation open no interval; a draft cancel keeps the reservation held and the accepted terms unchanged. A confirmed future-start order keeps its commitment reference on its reservation (SUB-D-100), apart from the accepted terms; confirmation or prepayment starts no service coverage.

The activation decision writes the first composition revision in the commit that samples T, under the subscription's lock: every lot of the accepted target at its full quantity, effective from T. A composition revision has no counter of its own: it is the aggregate revision of the commit that applied it, so the aggregate revision still moves once. Suspend, resume and cancel write no composition revision; the status revisions give their boundaries. A later revision only succeeds its predecessor. It keeps every lot of the predecessor under the same component at the same quantity and may add lots. It takes effect no earlier than its predecessor; at the same instant it leaves the predecessor an empty interval. The lots of one component always sum to a quantity of at most 28 significant digits, and no lot is collapsed into another. Each revision records its complete lot allocation and the digest of that allocation. Every composition table is append-only (DESIGN §3.7).

A subscription stored before compositions were typed holds no accepted terms, and none can be attached later: there is no backfill. It keeps its stored object and answers it unchanged, on REST as in the SDK. Its activation refuses `invalid_state` (`acceptance_missing`), stored (SUB-D-85). Its composition reads hold no component and no terms, and a served instant reads `evidence_missing` (SUB-D-110). A subscription that holds accepted terms was created with a typed composition in the same commit. So its stored composition must decode as `CompositionV1`; one that does not is a corrupt row (500) on every read and lock. A digest written before compositions were typed covers the raw composition object, and a typed request matches it only where that object was already in the canonical form (SUB-D-107). Otherwise a create applied before then matches no create retry: under another key it refuses `payload_changed`, stored, and under its own key the request decoder refuses the old body, or the key answers `idempotency_mismatch`. Such a create is recovered with the exact attempt lookup (`lookup_attempt`), which compares no digest, never by a create retry. Likewise a reserve of a line whose reservation was held before then, and the activation of a typed draft of that line, are judged as of another subject: `business_conflict` (`reservation_conflict`), stored (SUB-D-100, SUB-D-102). Such a reservation is released (SUB-D-101) and its purchase ordered again: the released line itself takes no new place (`reservation_released`).

**SUB-D-110: Composition as of service time.** Two reads answer the composition from frozen evidence only. `GET /subscriptions/v1/subscriptions/{id}/composition` takes an optional `at`, now when absent. It answers the components and lots held at `at`, the term slice that starts at `at` and the accepted terms they name. Before the first composition revision, the lots are the ones the create accepted, not yet applied. `GET /subscriptions/v1/subscriptions/{id}/composition/slices` takes `from` and `to`, both required, and answers the term slices of `[from, to)`. The SDK methods are `get_composition` and `get_composition_slices`. Both are reads by id: they ask `subscription_read` for the listed scope of the grant, and a subscription outside it answers 404 like an absent one (SUB-D-83). Each runs in one read-only snapshot (SUB-D-93) and names the aggregate revision it was taken at and each slice's `compositionRevision`. No owner is asked, Pricing and Products included.

Slices are half-open UTC intervals that cover the range once, in order. A slice holds one set of terms: its service state and, while served or suspended, the composition revision in force with each lot it allocates and the state of each binding. The service states are `served` (`active`), `suspended`, `before_service` (before the first T), `after_service` (from the cancel instant) and `evidence_missing`. A slice ends only where its terms change: the service state, the composition revision or a binding's state. An instant read is the one exception: when more changes follow `at` than one read takes, its slice ends at the last instant read, where the terms need not change (below). A status change that keeps the service state, such as `draft → preparing`, does not cut. So two adjacent slices always differ. A suspended slice keeps the revision and the lots in force. A binding ends at its own end, the instant `ends_on` 00:00:00Z, exclusive; an informational `effective_to` never cuts. From its end, with no continuation in this version, the binding reads `unresolved` (`binding_ended`) in every later slice. The lot's other bindings stay bound, and no other price stands in. A served instant without an applied composition revision reads `evidence_missing`, never a live price. Status changes are taken in revision order, each at the latest instant recorded up to it, so a clock that went back can neither reorder nor overlap slices. The payer is fixed, so there is no payer boundary.

A read takes at most 1,025 status changes and 1,025 composition revisions: one more than the 1,024 slices an answer holds. A read that took that many is cut at the last instant it read. A range that reaches past that instant, or holds more than 1,024 slices, is an invalid request on `to` (narrow the range). An instant read's slice ends at the last instant read. Before authorization, each instant must be RFC 3339 with a UTC form in the years 0 to 9999, and `to` must be after `from`; otherwise the read is an invalid request on that member. Each instant is read in UTC and floored to the millisecond (SUB-D-111), so a range whose two ends fall in one millisecond is an invalid request on `to`.

An answer carries each accepted terms record by its typed fields (acceptance, catalog, request digest, accepted instant, currency and region) and its `evidence_digest`, never the evidence document. DESIGN §3.3.8 gives this read each line's binding reference, not the document. Each lot of a slice carries its bindings as frozen: item, dimension value, price, money digest, SKU id, version and code, charge kind, usage policy reference, `ends_on` and state. The complete document is served only by a recovery read under `subscription_recover` (feature 09). A read decodes, and checks against its digest and its columns, only the documents of the terms whose lots the revisions it read allocate. Every allocation must name a lot of the subscription's accepted composition, under that lot's component; otherwise the row is corrupt (500).

**SUB-D-111: Instants recorded to the millisecond.** Every instant the gear records is floored to the millisecond once, at its source. The instants from which commercial facts take their boundaries are whole seconds (SUB-D-115); a whole second is also a whole millisecond, so the rules below hold for them. REST prints instants in milliseconds, so an instant that an answer prints reads back exactly. Each clock floors its now. An instant computed from a now is floored again: an activation deadline, an unpaused deadline, a back-off and a driver lease. A typed column copied from an owner holds the owner's instant floored to the millisecond: the accepted instant of accepted terms and the observed instant of a posture. A value frozen inside the evidence document stays as the owner gave it. A read floors the instants its caller names in the same way. A row stored with microseconds keeps them and still reads: nothing migrates it, and the store compares instants exactly. The composition reads take such an instant at its millisecond: they floor every status instant, every revision's effective instant and the last instant a cut read took before they cut slices (SUB-D-110). So a boundary prints and reads back as that millisecond, and an instant that an answer prints reads back exactly for these rows too. Changes inside one millisecond take effect together, the last in revision order in force, and no slice is empty. A requested start is not recorded as an instant; it is digested as the caller sent it.

**SUB-D-112: Provenance of an operation.** Every receipt and every audit record keeps who acted and through which grant. The audit of a command keeps apart the source user actor, whom Orders passes, the effective tenant (the ordering tenant), the confirmed order version (the origin version), the executing service principal and the request id ([commercial model §3.8](../../../../docs/arch/bss-commercial-model/DESIGN.md#38-orders-terms-and-subscriptions-contracts)). A service that acts under prior authority records its own execution, never a human confirmation that did not happen. The source user actor is an optional member of the create, the receiver commands and the reservation commands, which Orders supplies when it has one; the gear stores it as given, beside the executing principal, which is the asserting principal below. Until Orders sends it (S-U21), a command records no source user actor: a known gap. The asserting principal is the subject of the caller's security context: its id, its type when the context names one, and its home tenant. A type is recorded only when it is 1 to 128 visible ASCII characters; any other type is left out. Under the interim of SUB-D-125, the delegation evidence is the grant anchor: the anchor tenant of the decision point's answer that reaches the ordering tenant. A decision point that answers fenced to the target (S-U20) makes the anchor the ordering tenant itself. It is the ordering tenant itself when the grant is anchored there, otherwise the nearest ancestor that is an anchor and reaches it without crossing a self-managed barrier, from the same ancestors that the barrier check of SUB-D-83 walks. Only the ordering tenant's anchor is recorded; the questions about the other tenant axes of a create or a reserve stay admission checks. The anchor comes from the answer that the point question already reads, so no call is added. The platform security context carries no delegation reference and the decision point names none, so the anchor is the delegation evidence of SUB-D-76 until the platform supplies a verifiable reference. The receipt and the audit record keep the home tenant as `subject_tenant_id` beside the anchor as `scope_anchor_tenant_id`. The two are recorded together or not at all: a security context that names no home tenant records neither, and a stored receipt that holds one alone is read as a corrupt row. The receipt also keeps the correlation id that its request carried, and the audit record and the applied outcome take it from there. The receipt's provenance is fixed at admission. An activation resolved or closed after its request records the subject that admitted it as its authority, with the type, home tenant and anchor that its receipt keeps, never the gear's own service identity in its place (SUB-D-86, SUB-D-87). The executing principal of the drive is recorded beside it: the gear's service principal (SUB-D-126), or, until that exists, the first-party identity of SUB-D-123. A denial and a read write no receipt, so they record nothing (SUB-D-82). A receipt or an audit record written before the gear recorded provenance keeps none, and nothing backfills it. The applied outcome that `after_applied` receives (SUB-D-98) names the same actor and correlation id: `actor` (`ActorV1`: `subjectId`, `subjectType`, `subjectTenantId` and `scopeAnchorTenantId`) and `correlationId`. `subjectTenantId`, the subject's home tenant, pairs with `subjectId`: both name the subject, never the ordering tenant that it acted in. Both are optional members added within V1 and are left out when absent. An outcome whose actor has no recorded home tenant names no actor. An outcome queued before these members, and a reader that does not know them, still decode. Provenance is evidence, not authority: a recorded principal or anchor never authorizes a later command or read, and every request asks the decision point again (DESIGN §2.2). A revert of the provenance storage is refused while any receipt or audit record holds provenance. Its read and its drops run in one transaction, so no operation commits provenance between them; on PostgreSQL the revert takes its locks as SUB-D-113 states.

**SUB-D-113: Tenant axes fixed in storage.** The ordering, resource, payer and seller tenants of a subscription are written at create and never change. The repository's update writes only the status, the revision, T, the cancellation and the update instant. Behind it, a database trigger on both backends refuses any update that changes one of the four tenants, so no writer moves a subscription between tenants, past the repositories too. An update that writes a tenant unchanged passes. The other stored copies are guarded in the same way, past the repositories. A stored subscription is never deleted (on PostgreSQL never truncated either, on SQLite never replaced by an insert of its id), so no writer can re-home an id by a delete and an insert. Revision snapshots and audit records are append-only: an update or a delete of a row is refused on both backends, and a truncate on PostgreSQL. A receipt's ordering tenant and its provenance (subject, type, home tenant and grant anchor, SUB-D-112) are fixed once written; resolving the receipt writes none of them. So no stored copy of a tenant or of who acted can be rewritten. No purge exists in this version, so nothing removes these records. The purge after the financial-evidence retention (PRD record retention; DESIGN, posted-document immutability) will be the only path that removes them; it comes with its own migration and decision, which define how it passes these guards under a legal hold and the retention terms. Every revision snapshot keeps the tenants that it was written with, so the payer of a served interval is the one of its revision and no payer change splits a billing period. Tenant and payer transfer and import stay deferred, and their refusals need no surface. No route, SDK method or command names a new tenant for an existing subscription, and none imports one. A create from any origin but Orders, an import origin included, refuses `origin_disabled` with its receipt and audit record, before any subscription, fence, claim or owner call (SUB-D-71). No create adopts a subscription that it did not make. These guards and the provenance columns are removed by one revert, which is refused while provenance is held (SUB-D-112). On PostgreSQL the revert first bounds each lock wait with a timeout, then locks the subscription, revision, receipt and audit tables one after another, in the order a command writes them. That order does not prevent every cycle with a running command, because a command reads its receipt key before it locks the subscription. A deadlock with a command, or a lock wait past the timeout, aborts one side with nothing changed, and the revert can be run again. On SQLite the revert's transaction keeps every other writer out, or the revert fails.

**SUB-D-114: Hierarchy change after create.** A change of the tenant hierarchy after create moves nothing stored: a barrier placed, a tenant moved or a grant removed. The subscription, every revision snapshot, every receipt and every audit record keep the tenant axes and the provenance that they were written with (SUB-D-112, SUB-D-113). Authority follows the current hierarchy: every command and read is authorized again as SUB-D-83 states, against the hierarchy as it stands. The decision of an activation can come well after its admission, so the activation is checked again before its decision commits. The drive that decides it, the request's own drive or the driver's, asks Tenant Resolver again after the `before_apply` stage allows: without the decision point, outside any transaction and under the provider call timeout, as the admission asked it. The ordering tenant must still be active, and, while the interim of SUB-D-125 records a grant anchor, the anchor that the receipt recorded must still be the tenant itself or one of its ancestors, with no self-managed barrier crossed. A tenant that is no longer active or no longer known closes the activation `tenant_inactive`; an anchor that no longer reaches it closes the activation `policy_denied`. Either closure holds only while the decision version is still the one that the drive read. A receipt written before receipts recorded the anchor is checked for the tenant's standing only; nothing stands in for an anchor that was not recorded. A resolver that cannot answer, or does not answer in time, is an unavailable dependency: the activation waits `dependency_unavailable` within its deadline and is never closed for it. A change of the hierarchy between this check and the commit is not seen. The closure's audit record names the subject that admitted the activation, with the anchor that its receipt recorded (SUB-D-112). A caller whose grant no longer reaches the ordering tenant is refused as any caller outside it: 404 for activation, a lifecycle command, an exact lookup and a read by id, and 403 for a create, a receiver command and a reservation command. A teardown is still judged by the barrier check; only the test that the tenant is active is skipped. A principal of the ordering tenant itself still reads and cancels its subscription. Tenant ancestry alone never authorizes: Tenant Resolver only narrows an allow of the decision point, and a missing or unavailable answer is never a guessed identity or permission.

**SUB-D-115: Commercial instants are whole seconds.** The `subscriptions-rating/1` profile (SUB-D-117) reads only whole UTC seconds, so every instant that can become a period, slice or served boundary of a fact is a whole second from its source. Activation T is the decision's instant floored to the whole second. The decision samples that instant once and judges its deadline and the staged evidence at the instant itself, never at the floored T, so a decision later in the second of its deadline, or of an evidence expiry, is past it. An applied lifecycle command (`suspend`, `resume`, `cancel`, `archive`) samples its instant at the whole second, so its revision and its cancellation instant are whole seconds. Every subscription revision is recorded at the whole second: a create floors its revision and the composition it freezes (the component, terms and lot instants), admission floors its preparing revision, and every closure of an activation floors its revision, whatever closes it (deadline, veto, definitive failure, key movement, revoke, settle or preparing cancel). The reason is the slice timeline: it takes each status change at the latest instant recorded up to it in revision order, so one finer instant moves every later boundary off the second. For example, a create at S+0.700 followed by a `no_preparation` activation in the same second would move T to S+0.700 and block the period for good. The period reconciler samples its now at the whole second for discovery, the backlog and every row it writes (SUB-D-119). The instants of a create and of a closure that no boundary reads keep the millisecond of SUB-D-111: the subscription's create and update instants, receipts, fences, ledger rows, audit records, deadlines and the typed columns copied from owners. A whole second is also a whole millisecond, so every rule of SUB-D-111 holds for these instants. An instant frozen in accepted evidence stays as the owner gave it (SUB-D-111). When the anchor or start of a frozen billing schedule is finer than a second, no period is derived from a truncated value: the cursor blocks `sub_second_instant` (SUB-D-118). This amends SUB-D-111.

**SUB-D-116: Fact identity, versions and digests.** A fact is one obligation in one commercial period. Its `fact_id` is the UUIDv5, in the namespace `5b0c7e4a-2d1f-4c8e-9a3b-6e2f1d4c8a70`, of the canonical JSON text `["fact",1,subscriptionId,componentId,itemId,dimensionValue,kind,periodStart]`: `null` for an item priced without a dimension, and the period start as a whole-second RFC 3339 UTC instant. No price, lot, purchase or occurrence is part of it, so a new price, an added lot or a retried action never makes a second fact (SUB-D-19, SUB-D-21). The billing group of a period is the UUIDv5, in the namespace `5b0c7e4a-2d1f-4c8e-9a3b-6e2f1d4c8a71`, of `["group",1,subscriptionId,sellerTenantId,payerTenantId,currency,periodStart]`. Versions are insert-only and start at 1. Version 1 names no predecessor; a higher version names the version below it as `predecessor_version`. `payload_digest` is the lowercase hex SHA-256 of the canonical JSON text of the document without its `payload_digest` member: members sorted by name in byte order at every depth, no whitespace, and scalars as JSON prints them. Rating's own profile fixtures use the same codec, so a consumer can verify the digest ([fact intake](UPSTREAM_REQS.md#requested-fact-intake)). The content digest is the same function over the document without `fact_version`, `predecessor_version`, `source_commercial_revision` and `payload_digest`. It is stored and never published. A new version is written only when the content digest of the derived document differs from that of its head, so a period derived again with unchanged content writes nothing. A version stored again with the same payload digest is a replay: it writes no journal entry and no event. A version stored again with another payload digest is a conflict: nothing is written, the conflict is logged at error and counted on `bss_subscriptions_fact_conflicts_total`, and the whole commit fails as an internal error. Any other failure of the insert is not counted as a conflict.

**SUB-D-117: The fact document is Rating's `subscriptions-rating/1` profile.** Every fact version is one JSON document of the profile `subscriptions-rating/1`, and Rating's fact reader owns that profile. The reader refuses a member it does not know, so the document carries exactly the snake_case members of [DESIGN §3.3.3](DESIGN.md#commercial-fact-envelope); an absent optional member means not applicable. A fact is derived only from frozen accepted evidence and committed effective-dated history, never from a live read. The gear writes one fact per obligation key, the component, item, dimension and charge kind that a lot holds in a served or suspended slice of the period; a period with no such slice writes none. The served extent runs from the first to the last slice that holds the key. Its slices are contiguous, and adjacent spans with the same content are merged. A span in which every lot holds a bound binding is `chargeable`, with `reason: null`, exactly one terms reference and its recurring lots. A suspended span is `unresolved` with the reason `suspension_posture_unaccepted`. A span whose binding has ended is `unresolved` with the reason `binding_ended`. Unresolved spans name no terms and carry their recurring lots with `terms_ref: null`. A span inside the extent in which the key holds no lot is `nonchargeable` with the reason `not_held` and no lots. Usage facts carry no lots in any slice; Rating schedules their windows (SUB-D-32). Accepted terms carry no suspension bill/waive posture in this version (S-U08), and the PRD forbids inferring one from status. So no `suspensions` member is written, and Rating holds a fact with a suspended span pending and does not price it. Once a posture is accepted, a successor version carries the suspension ranges and chargeable spans. A recurring lot's `unit` is the unit of its binding, or the code of the bound dated SKU when the binding names none: that is what the quantity counts. No `occurrence` member is written, because one-time charges are a closed capability (SUB-D-122). A fact that the period no longer holds gets a `zeroed` successor version: `obligation` `zeroed`, `served` empty at the period start, no slices, no terms and no suspensions. Nothing is deleted. Frozen evidence that cannot meet a requirement of Rating's reader blocks the period instead of producing a fact that Rating would quarantine (SUB-D-118). These block `terms_unsupported`: a binding not approved in a chargeable span; two timings, meters, usage policies or currencies under one key; two terms records in one chargeable span of one key; a catalog tenant other than the seller; terms with no Rating form, such as a calendar-hour rating window outside UTC; and a blank lot unit. A document whose canonical text exceeds 56 KiB blocks `fact_too_large`, so its event stays within the outbox's 64 KiB payload limit and never fails the commit (SUB-D-121). The money, template and policy digests are carried as Pricing froze them. The conformance evidence of the profile is a set of fixture documents that the gear keeps: the versions that an activation writes (`active`), the versions that a suspend writes (`suspended`), the newest versions after a suspend, a resume and a cancel (`ended`), zeroed versions (`zeroed`), the versions that the reconciler writes for the periods it repairs (`repaired`), and the binding document of every price model that a fact can carry (`bindings`). Rating's reader accepted every document when it was written. A test of the gear builds each document again from the same flows and fails when the gear would write another one. It computes each payload digest again by the codec of SUB-D-116, so a change of the digest's form also fails it. The gear does not depend on Rating's code: Rating is asked to accept the same fixtures in its own fact-intake test ([fact intake](UPSTREAM_REQS.md#requested-fact-intake)), so a change on either side that breaks the profile fails a test.

**SUB-D-118: Period cursor.** Each subscription has one period cursor. It is `open`, with the frozen schedule, the start of the period last opened, the index and start of the next period and its due instant; `finished`, when no later period has service; or `blocked`, with a visible reason and the instant it blocked. The schedule is read once from the frozen accepted billing terms and copied into the cursor when it opens: `cycle` (month or year), `anchor` (calendar or subscription start), `anchor_at` and the UTC timezone, with the accepted start and term (SUB-D-27). A stored cursor schedule with a fixed term of no periods does not decode, so the cursor never holds one. Period n is `anchor_at + n × cycle`. Each period is computed from the anchor, with its day clamped to the last day of a shorter month, so it never drifts. Publication runs inside the lifecycle commit, after every other member of that commit, under the subscription lock that the command already holds. The activation that makes a subscription active opens the period that contains T and inserts the cursor. A suspend, resume or cancel of an active or suspended subscription derives again the open period that it falls in. A change dated before the open period takes effect at the open period's start: the open period is derived again, the earlier period that the change falls in is not, and the change is warned once the commit lands. A change at or after the end of the open period waits for the reconciler (SUB-D-119). A period that cannot be derived never refuses the lifecycle command: the cursor becomes `blocked` with its reason, and the lifecycle commit goes on. The warning and the `blocked` count follow only once that commit lands, so a rolled-back commit reports nothing. A stored row that a publication read cannot decode also blocks the period, as `evidence_missing` or `history_missing` below, and never refuses the command. A storage failure, or a cursor row that cannot be decoded, fails the transaction, as any other write does. The block reasons are: `evidence_missing` (frozen evidence or committed history absent, or a stored row of it that does not decode, such as an accepted-evidence record whose billing-terms digest is not lowercase hex); `schedule_missing` (no billing schedule in a terms record whose document the read takes, or one this version cannot read: another cycle, anchor or timezone, a fixed term of no periods, or an anchor or start without a UTC form; a read that takes no such record is `evidence_missing`); `schedule_unsupported` (two terms records with different schedules, a terms record bound to another schedule, an activation in a period before the accepted start, or a period index with no calendar form); `sub_second_instant` (SUB-D-115); `one_time_charge` (SUB-D-122); `too_many_changes` (a period holding more status changes or composition revisions than one read takes, SUB-D-110); `term_ended` (an activation past the last period of a fixed term); `history_missing` (a change of an activated subscription that has no cursor, a stored fact version of the period that does not decode, or a subscription whose service had started when the commercial stores were created, whatever its status: the migration that creates them gives each such subscription a blocked cursor at the migration's instant, in whole seconds, because its earlier periods were never published); and `terms_unsupported` and `fact_too_large` (SUB-D-117). A blocked cursor is never retried by guessing; it stays in the blocked count. The end of service finishes the cursor. A cancel takes effect at the instant the slice timeline gives it: the latest instant recorded up to it in revision order (SUB-D-115). A cancel derives the open period again, and it finishes the cursor only when that period's history ends service. So a cancel that takes effect before the open period zeroes that period's facts and finishes the cursor, and one that takes effect after the open period leaves the cursor open. A period that lies past the last period of a fixed term, or at whose start the status in force, as the history gives it, is cancelled or archived, finishes the cursor when it falls due (SUB-D-119); a renewal opens a new term. A cancel or an archive finishes a blocked cursor only when its block holds nothing billable: a `term_ended` block, or a block whose earliest possible period had no chargeable service before the end of service. That period is the period last opened, else the next one, else the one in which service started; the end of service is the cancel's instant, and for an archive the instant of its cancellation. Otherwise the cursor stays blocked and counted, and once the commit lands it warns that service ended with a period that had service and is not published. An archive leaves an open cursor to the reconciler and inserts none. Under the subscription lock the commercial rows are written in this order: the fact versions, the ordering tenant's journal head (only when a version is written), then the cursor, which records how the period ended (SUB-D-90).

**SUB-D-119: Period reconciler.** A worker beside the activation driver opens the next period of each subscription when it is due, and repairs the periods missed while the gear was down. It runs as the gear's own service principal (SUB-D-123), holds no lease and calls no provider. A pass reads at most `batch` open cursors that are due, oldest due first, across every tenant. It repairs at most `concurrency` subscriptions at a time, each up to `catch_up` periods. Each period is one short transaction within the budget of one drive (SUB-D-87). The transaction locks the subscription, reads the cursor again and stops when the next period has not started. It finishes the cursor when the status in force at that period's start, as the history gives it, is cancelled or archived (SUB-D-118), or when the period lies past the last period of a fixed term. Otherwise it derives and publishes the period by the process of the lifecycle commit (SUB-D-116 to SUB-D-118), so a stored row that the period's read cannot decode blocks the cursor here too, and moves the cursor only from the index it read. So two passes, or two replicas, open each period once: the loser finds it no longer due, or is counted `skipped`. A period that this replica loses to another writer twice in a row at the same index is no lost race: it is warned, counted `failed` and backs its cursor off as a failed period does. Each replica keeps only the last lost index of each cursor that its last pass found due. A budget per period, not per subscription, keeps the periods already committed and their counts. A stop before a subscription's first period counts it `skipped`; a stop during its catch-up keeps what was opened and leaves the rest due. A period whose opening fails, runs out of its budget or panics is logged with its causes and counted (`failed` or `panicked`), and its cursor backs off in a transaction of its own. The back-off finds the cursor by its key and the index of the failed period, and writes only while the cursor is open at that index and still counts the failures read. It writes `attempts + 1`, a due instant of `now + min(2^(attempts − 1) s, 15 min)` in whole seconds without jitter, and the write instant. It is the one cursor write without the subscription's lock (SUB-D-90): it never moves the cursor, never delays a cursor that another writer moved, and counts one failure once when two back-offs race. A failed period whose cursor another writer moved on, or backed off, before its back-off read it is not backed off again and is not left due, and its back-off is logged at debug. A cursor that another writer moved on first counts as moved, so a full batch whose cursors were all moved by other writers also runs the next pass at once. A back-off that the database refuses because it is busy is not such a case: it is a failed repair, its cursor is left due, and it waits as any failure does. A subscription or cursor row that cannot be decoded still backs off. A due cursor whose subscription is not found, and an open cursor with no next start, are failures of this kind. Any move of the cursor resets `attempts`. At the tenth failure in a row the commit warns once and counts `stuck`. The waits before it, from one second doubling, add up to 511 s, so that failure comes about eight and a half minutes after the first, within the fifteen-minute repair budget. The cursor is still tried, at most fifteen minutes apart. After a pass that filled its batch and moved (opened, finished or blocked) or backed off a cursor, the next pass runs at once. After a pass that moved and backed off nothing while a failure left a cursor due, the next pass runs a poll interval later, so a database that refuses writes is not retried in a loop. Otherwise the next pass runs when the earliest open cursor falls due, from 1 s to a poll interval later, and a poll interval later when no cursor is open or that read fails. A failed pass waits the activation driver's back-off, raised to at least a poll interval and held to at most fifteen minutes. Each discovery read of a pass, of the due cursors, of the next due instant and of the backlog, runs within the budget of one drive (SUB-D-87). A due read that fails or runs past it fails the pass, counted `failed`; a next due read that does makes the next pass wait a poll interval; a backlog read that does is warned. Replicas do not share the work; each takes the oldest due cursors. After its repairs, a pass reports the backlog: the age of the oldest period that has started and is not opened on an open cursor, backed off or not, and the number of blocked cursors. A pass whose discovery failed still reports the backlog. A failed read of the backlog is warned and does not fail the pass. The opening lag, from the start of a period to its opening by the reconciler, is recorded once that opening commits. A blocked cursor is counted once, where it is blocked (SUB-D-118). The settings and the metrics are in [DESIGN §3.8](DESIGN.md#38-deployment-topology). With the defaults, a pass runs at least every 60 s and catches up 12 missed periods of a subscription; the 15-minute repair budget holds only while one replica drains its queue in time, and remains S-Q07 evidence to measure.

**SUB-D-120: Recovery journal.** Every new fact version appends one entry to the commercial journal of its ordering tenant, in the transaction that writes the version: the position, subscription, fact, version, payload digest and recording instant. Positions start at 1 and come from one head row per ordering tenant. The head is inserted if absent, locked, and moved forward only from the position read, so the published high watermark never skips an uncommitted lower position ([DESIGN §3.7](DESIGN.md#37-database-schemas--tables)). A replay writes no entry. Entries are append-only and the head only moves forward: database triggers refuse an update or a delete of an entry on both backends and a truncate on PostgreSQL, and a delete of the head, its truncate on PostgreSQL, or any move of it backward or to another tenant. A revert of these stores is refused while any fact version exists. On PostgreSQL it bounds each lock wait and locks the period cursor, the fact versions, the journal head and the journal, in that order: the order in which every commercial writer reaches them, the cursor first. So the revert meets a writer in a cycle only when that writer reached a later table before the cursor; the database then aborts one side, or the revert's lock wait runs out, and the aborted side changes nothing. The journal is written whether or not events are published, so a correction to an old period can be discovered after every event is lost (feature 08 §3.1). Seal versions join the journal with the seals. The catch-up and snapshot reads over it are those of feature 09.

**SUB-D-121: Fact events through the gear's outbox, off by default.** The only event published in this version is `BillableFactPublished`, one per new fact version, delivered at least once. Lifecycle, intent, transition-outcome, posture, seal and attribution events come with their capabilities, and no event of a closed capability is part of the contract. The event contract is a set of GTS inventory entries: the Subscriptions topic instance, the abstract Subscriptions family and its final type `billable_fact_published` ([DESIGN §4.2](DESIGN.md#events-and-topics)). The fact subject type is registered at the gear's init with its authorization labels, and a refused registration fails the boot. The event's `data` carries the family's common members, then `factId`, `factVersion`, `payloadDigest` and `fact`: the complete stored `subscriptions-rating/1` document of the version. So a consumer hands `fact` to Rating's intake without reading it back. The final type restates every common member with the family's exact schema and closes `data`. It restates `subscriptionId` because the broker checks the partition key against the type's own `data`. `fact` stays open, because its shape is for Rating's reader to check. The subject is `<factId>/v<version>`, the envelope tenant is the producer's platform-root tenant, and the partition key is `/data/subscriptionId`. `correlation` is the operation of the lifecycle commit that wrote the version, or the fact ID when the reconciler wrote it. The event is enqueued on the queue `bss-subscriptions-facts` of the gear's transactional outbox, on the partition of its subscription, in the transaction that writes its version, so a rolled-back commit leaves no event and a committed version always has one. `commercial.events.enabled` is `false` by default: nothing is enqueued and no broker is asked. When it is enabled, `producer_subject_id` and `producer_tenant_id` (the platform root) are required and not nil, and the producer subject needs the broker's grant to produce the family in that tenant. The producer's context is that of the gear's service principal (SUB-D-126). Until the client-credentials exchange exists, the context is built in the process from these two settings: a known gap, closed by default because the events are off. `serve` registers the fact queue on the gear's outbox with no worker, so nothing leases its partitions while the producer is not bound, and an event waits there, at no cost, however long the producer takes to bind. The producer binds in the background, beside the workers, and no command or worker waits for it. Each attempt takes the broker and the tenant resolver from ClientHub again and has at most 30 s. It first asks the tenant resolver for the platform root, before any broker call: a root that is nil, has a parent or is not active, or that is not `producer_tenant_id`, is refused and logged at error from the first attempt. Any other failed attempt, including a missing broker or one that does not know the contract yet, is warned, and logged at error from the tenth attempt on. Every failed attempt is retried after a fully jittered back-off of 250 ms, doubling up to 30 s, until the producer binds or the gear stops. Once bound, the producer starts a pipeline of its own over the gear's outbox tables: its queue `bss-subscriptions-events`, with the outbox's configured partitions, and a relay of the fact queue. A pipeline that fails to start is logged at error, and the producer binds again. The relay hands each event to the producer in a transaction of its own, in the order of its subscription, wakes the producer's queue once that transaction commits, and then acknowledges the event. From then on a commit writes its fact event through the producer's pipeline and wakes that pipeline only once the commit lands, so a commit never combines the wakes of two outboxes. A replica whose producer is not bound still commits, and its events wait; the relay of a bound replica hands them over within its idle poll, the 30 s outbox lease. A relay cut between its transaction and its acknowledgement hands the event over again, so the broker can hold one fact version twice, under two event IDs. A consumer takes a repeat of a version that it holds, with the same payload digest, as a replay (SUB-D-116). A fact event whose data the producer refuses is dead-lettered and logged at error. Its version stays committed and journaled, so it reaches a consumer only through the recovery reads of feature 09. An event of another payload type, or one that does not decode, is dead-lettered the same way; any other failure of the relay is warned and retried, and the outbox's attempt count must never turn such a failure into a stopped partition (UPSTREAM_REQS §2.2). While the gear's outbox is not running, before `serve` starts it and after `serve` stops it, a commit that would enqueue a fact event or an applied outcome is refused `dependency_unavailable` and stores nothing (SUB-D-82). A producer that is not bound never refuses a commit. A failed enqueue fails the commit. At stop, `serve` stops the workers first, then the gear's outbox, so no commit enqueues any more; then the producer lets go of its pipeline, and the pipeline stops with its relay. An event still on the fact queue waits for the next start. Fact events already in the outbox when the events are turned off stay there, and are relayed once the events are on again and the producer binds; while the events are off, no new fact event is written. The facts and the journal are written whether the events are enabled or not.

**SUB-D-122: One-time charges are a closed capability.** A create whose accepted Pricing terms hold any binding that charges once refuses `capability_closed` (`one_time_charges`) before its transaction and stores nothing (SUB-D-82). The occurrence schema (S-Q04) and the one-time routing and accounting (S-U08, S-U13) are still open, so no fact carries `kind` `one_time` or an `occurrence` member. A one-time binding already in stored evidence blocks its period `one_time_charge` (SUB-D-118).

**SUB-D-123: System worker identity.** The activation driver, the posture worker and the period reconciler work without a request, so no caller's context authorizes what they do. They run as the gear's one service principal (SUB-D-126). Only these named workers discover work. A discovery pages an authorized roster of tenant references, at most 200 references a page, and each read or write of a row's data then obtains the single-target scope of its tenant through the guard of SUB-D-125 ([commercial model §3.4](../../../../docs/arch/bss-commercial-model/DESIGN.md#34-internal-dependencies)). No worker builds a scope or holds a scope over the data of every tenant, not even to discover work. The platform does not provide the client-credentials exchange, the account, its roles or the roster yet (UPSTREAM_REQS §2.2, S-U20). Until it does, the workers use the gear's discovery warrant: a discovery reads every tenant, but only the ordering tenant and the ids of each row, in pages of the worker's configured batch rather than the roster bound, and every read and write that follows runs under the scope of that one tenant. A drive asks its providers as a first-party identity of the gear, built in the process and logged at each construction. This interim is a known gap, gated by the service principal of UPSTREAM_REQS §2.2 and the roster of S-U20. This amends SUB-D-87, SUB-D-106 and SUB-D-119.

**SUB-D-124: A named consumer of Accounts.** Accounts owns billing accounts, legal parties, issuer bindings, account-use arrangements and account-use grants ([ADR 0002](../../docs/ADR/0002-cpt-cf-bss-adr-commercial-ownership-tenant-rbac.md), [Accounts DESIGN](../../accounts/docs/DESIGN.md), both proposed). Account Management holds tenant identity and hierarchy only. It is never a source of commercial accounts, relationship evidence or consent. Subscriptions takes the Subscriptions row of the [commercial model's owner reconciliation](../../../../docs/arch/bss-commercial-model/DESIGN.md#41-owner-reconciliation-before-adoption) and the Accounts consumer contract as its own decisions. This decision is the Subscriptions owner's acceptance of that row, which ADR 0002 names as a condition of its acceptance. The rules:

- The gear's service principal (SUB-D-126) holds narrow Accounts roles: authorize a use, record its outcome, release, end, and read status.
- Before a use that charges an account (a renewal, a quantity increase, or a payer or account transfer), the gear calls `authorize_use` and records the outcome. It allocates a durable, monotonic `use_version` per subscription, leg and use kind. It never changes the grant of Orders. The first purchase is the use of Orders: its grant is evidence that the create carries.
- The gear records the billing assignment of each leg: segments keyed by `(leg_id, assignment_version)`, with the account, payer, seller, issuer, debtor, currency and pinned Ledger book, and the grant as evidence. It records a segment after activation, from the actual service start, with the calendar interval of the accepted term ([commercial model §3.8](../../../../docs/arch/bss-commercial-model/DESIGN.md#38-orders-terms-and-subscriptions-contracts)). Accounts owns no assignment.
- Period facts and assignment segments carry the resale `chain_version` ([ADR 0003](../../docs/ADR/0003-cpt-cf-bss-adr-multi-tier-resale-model.md)).
- At termination the gear publishes a durable receipt, keyed by the admitted order version and the line or leg, and delivers it at least once through its outbox, so that Orders can end its grant.
- The gear serves an `assignment_status` read and maintains the payer leg view. The source policy has no payer predicate. Users of the resource tenant never see upstream legs.
- A supply gap and an unavailable authority deny readiness under their own causes, never as nonpayment, inside the existing lifecycle contracts. Nothing terminates automatically.
- The quota entitlement (feature 05) is the enforcing mechanism of `quota_controlled` funding.
- Buyer, debtor party, payer account, document recipients and payment instruments are five separate facts. The gear takes the payer account and the debtor from the assignment and never infers one fact from another.
- An attachment (upstream) order never creates a subscription: a create whose origin is of the attachment category refuses before any effect.
- A payer or account transfer takes effect only at a shared period boundary, with `authorize_use` on the target account and the same seller, issuer, debtor and currency, and it carries the leg and account evidence. Transfer stays deferred (SUB-D-113); this is the rule that it meets when it is built.

This version delivers none of these rules. The consumer contract, the assignment, the chain version, the payer leg view, `assignment_status`, the five facts, the readiness causes and transfer wait on S-U19, and the per-leg facts also on the Rating contract of S-U08. What Orders supplies (it consumes the termination receipt to end its grant, marks the attachment category and passes the source user actor) waits on S-U21. The quota link waits on S-U04. Until Accounts is delivered, the relationship evidence of a purchase is the accepted Pricing receipt checked against the create (SUB-D-108), and no create waits on Accounts. This amends SUB-D-71 and SUB-D-113.

**SUB-D-125: Scopes from the decision point, guarded.** Every call that names a tenant obtains a single-target scope from the policy decision point: the target is the context tenant, the tenant mode is root-only and constraints are required ([commercial model §3.4](../../../../docs/arch/bss-commercial-model/DESIGN.md#34-internal-dependencies)). A returned-scope guard checks every answer before the gear uses it: every branch must be fenced to `owner_tenant_id` equal to the target. An answer fenced to the target is used unchanged. A set of several owner tenants, a subtree predicate, an unknown predicate or a branch without an owner fence is refused as an authority or configuration error, never as a denial: a 503 `ServiceUnavailable` refusal, logged and counted for the operator. There is no fallback. The gear passes the verified scope unchanged to storage. It never builds, narrows or widens a scope and has no scope that allows every tenant. This holds for the commands and lookups, the workers (SUB-D-123), the Pricing read (SUB-D-108) and the calls to Accounts (SUB-D-124). User callers keep tenant barriers. A named service principal crosses a self-managed barrier only through an explicitly authorized internal service path, never because a request asks for it. A read by id of a service caller is a point read that names its target tenant; a list of the tenants that a grant reaches serves user callers only. The guard and the point reads are part of this version. This holds above 10,000 tenants: it is tested at 10,001 tenants, with a target of 100,000 ([Accounts PRD](../../accounts/docs/PRD.md)).

The deployed decision point does not meet this yet: it answers a root-only question with the anchor tenant of the grant that reaches the target, not with the target (S-U20). Until S-U20 is met, an answer fenced only to other tenants, which is that anchor answer, takes an interim path, a known gap. After the checks of SUB-D-83, the gear builds the command scope `owner_tenant_id IN [T]` from that allow and records the anchor as SUB-D-112 states. It refuses a service caller whose ordering, payer or seller tenant is behind a self-managed barrier. This amends SUB-D-83, SUB-D-112 and SUB-D-114.

**SUB-D-126: One service principal for the gear.** The gear has one service principal: a service account homed in the platform-root tenant, with narrow action and resource roles assigned at the platform root. Its security context comes only from the client-credentials exchange of the AuthN Resolver, never from a context that the gear builds. A role at the root says where the principal may ask; it is not a scope, and every call still obtains the single-target scope of SUB-D-125. This one principal runs the system workers (SUB-D-123), reads Pricing acceptance (SUB-D-108), produces the fact events (SUB-D-121) and calls Accounts (SUB-D-124). A read of Pricing acceptance is a pinned read in the catalog of an explicit tenant: the seller's, taken from validated receipt evidence, under that tenant's single-target scope, through Pricing's service read path with registered service permissions (commercial model §3.4 and the Products and Pricing row of its §4.1). Pricing's acceptance read evaluates the decision at the subject's own tenant and names no context tenant, so it refuses a principal homed at the platform root until Pricing provides that path, with the catalog tenant as the context tenant. A principal for each seller tenant is not the model: per-tenant service assignments are a future alternative and never replace the single-target scope of each call. Until the platform provides the exchange, the account and its roles, and Pricing its service read path (UPSTREAM_REQS §2.2), every create refuses `capability_closed` (`accepted_terms`, 501) and stores nothing, the workers run on the interim of SUB-D-123, and the event producer's context is built in the process (SUB-D-121). These are known gaps. This amends SUB-D-108, SUB-D-121 and SUB-D-123.

**SUB-D-127: Service-only commands are not public routes.** Create, the receiver commands (`settle`, `pause`, `unpause`, `revoke`), the reservation commands (`reserve`, `release`) and the two recovery lookups of Orders (the exact attempt lookup and the exact reservation lookup) are service-only. They are SDK methods only and are not mounted in the public router, as Accounts does for its own service-only methods ([Accounts DESIGN §3.8](../../accounts/docs/DESIGN.md#38-sdk-and-management-contracts)). The gear checks that the caller is the principal named for that action: the Orders service principal, and for the incident release of SUB-D-101 a named operator principal. A user cannot call one of these methods by naming a source id ([commercial model §3.3](../../../../docs/arch/bss-commercial-model/DESIGN.md#33-api-contracts), §3.8). The caller check runs when a deployment configures the Orders service principal for the gear. Until it does, only policy limits who calls these SDK methods: their permissions go to the Orders service account only (SUB-D-96). This is a known gap, gated by the Orders principal provisioned for the gear (S-U01). The request id of an SDK call comes from its trace context. This amends SUB-D-77, SUB-D-96, SUB-D-100 and SUB-D-101.

**Metered-usage allocation.** A usage component may be added, or its included allowance increased, when the meter scope is the whole subscription and the whole component keeps one accepted binding. An increase that needs allocation of metered usage or tier qualification between purchase lots or between subscriptions refuses before effects until the S-U08 quantity-lot allocation contract covers metered usage.

Financial admission binds the exact acquisition/operation and accepted terms. Initial activation consumes the retained allocated payment or durable future postpay commitment, without another debit or credit assessment. Technical proof expiry and later risk/limit changes do not revoke that basis. Explicit applicable financial dispositions are versioned and fenced against local application; missing evidence is recovered under the same acquisition. Ledger accounting is evidence, not admission authority.

Orders Lifecycle owns durable capture-start arbitration against acquisition cancellation after place and terms are protected. Workflow transports and recovers; Payments deduplicates the stable capture identity. Cancellation winning prevents late debit. Capture winning preserves cancellation/remedy rights. Unknown capture keeps protection; release requires authoritative resolution and capture/activation fences with the applicable remedy, not an assumed refund amount or timing. Multi-line outcomes retain per-line obligations; successful lines are not automatically cancelled by another line's failure.

Explicit compensation has its own authorization and evidence.
